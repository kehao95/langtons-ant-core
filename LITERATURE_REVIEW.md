# Literature and contributions

The comparisons below identify the mathematical problems, their closest cited
predecessors and the contribution of each article. Source links point to the
primary literature and fixed versions used by the papers.

| Article | Contribution | Relationship to prior work |
|---|---|---|
| **Four-row 0.1.0** | Complete persistent-response closure and an all-width certificate for `S(4,n)=34n−150` for `n≥13`, with a reproducible exact classification at smaller widths. | Etse already gives the `34n−150` lower construction for `n≥14` and finite computations. The paper credits that family and numerical evidence and supplies the all-width exact upper proof. The resolution of his published Conjecture 11 is accurately stated. |
| **Factor 0.1.0** | Concrete ten-factor eventual modes, all-parameter fold exclusion, sharp transient slope 12, exact J10 macros, finite-horizon supply and the sharp nine-factor mass-window threshold. | Symbolic/forbidden-word methods and trace-language/return-tour connections are prior work; the physical fold exclusion is specific to this language. Nine is a mass threshold; ten concerns eventual attraction on arbitrary colourings. The generalized-ant reference is the 2025 journal article. |
| **Defect 0.1.0** | Complete mutable-field realization combined with a complete bounded-defect schema cover; explicit ant resource spectra; polynomial-size physical encoding for the stated strides; unary-budget small witnesses and NP/FP^NP upper bounds. | Presburger–semilinear equivalence, unary counting, piecewise-affine functions, flat-run QF encodings and integer small solutions are prior arithmetic tools. The supply dichotomy is a generic consequence of a semilinear resource image. The physical reduction and exact fold–consumer spectrum carry the ant-specific contribution. |
| **Memory 0.1.0** | Total next-old-black and birth-filtered queries with exact full-state output; quadratic event budgets and geometry; quantitative remote renewal necessity; a twenty-black, fixed-event-count lifetime obstruction. | Infinite memory effects are already discussed in symbolic-ant work. The new queries concern specific events, with fixed-D/fixed-filter complexity, rather than arbitrary-cell visitation. The generalized-ant journal article and original complexity paper provide the comparison. |
| **Wake 0.3.0** | Conditional uniqueness at drift `(2,−2)`, growth 12, width 10 and interior thinness, plus general prescribed-wake inverse synthesis and the integrated parity refinements. | The cited Jillhewar revision already excludes diagonal widths 2, 4, 6 and 8. Its `W≥10` result leaves the width addressed by the new conditional uniqueness theorem. Realizability, extremal rigidity and the inherited rank factorization retain their attribution. |
| **Port 0.2.0** | Sharp four-cell one-sided interface and 918-update three-cell bound; four-cell completion of arbitrary finite observations; protected finite scattering and return composition with complete history. | Earlier finite circuits already compose gadgets and finish in a highway seed. The new results give the minimal resource threshold and a specific full-field closure theorem. |

The four-row theorem concerns ordinary finite rectangles with arbitrary initial
colours and internal poses. Jillhewar's transverse-width results concern
periodic plane highways and extremal guard rails. Their uses of “width four”
describe different domains and must not be interchanged.

The factor/memory event algorithms answer specific predicates on supplied finite
inputs. The defect theorem quantifies over coordinates at supplied budgets.
Neither is a claim that every reachability or eventual-periodicity problem for
the classical ant is decidable. Generalized multicolour rules and torus boundary
conditions are also separate models.
