\section{Five rows and autonomous persistent memory}\label{sec:five}
Let $S(h,n)$ be the largest first-exit time from an $h$-row, $n$-column
rectangle, over all initial colors and all internal poses. The exit-producing
update is counted. Exterior cells are never read before this exit and may be
arbitrary; our constructions set them white.

\begin{theorem}[Quadratic five-row confinement]\label{five:thm:quadratic}
For every $k\in\N$, an explicitly given complete finite coloring has $10k+23$
black cells in a five-row rectangle of width $n=4k+9$. From its specified pose,
the first-exit time is
\[
 T_k=8k^2+60k+16=\tfrac12 n^2+6n-\tfrac{157}{2}.
\]
The trajectory crosses its central vertical seam exactly $2k$ times. Its complete
coloring after each round and at exit is given explicitly. Consequently
$S(h,n)=\Omega(n^2)$ for each fixed $h\ge5$.
\end{theorem}

This is an exact formula for the constructed family, not for the maximum
$S(5,n)$. It answers the concluding fixed-height linear-bound question
of~\cite{etse}, while retaining that paper's polynomial upper bound.
Together with the linear bounds through height four~\cite{etse} and Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, five
is the least height admitting superlinear worst-case first escape.

A second result concerns a deliberately modest observable. A block is repeatedly
entered from the same vertical side. Colors persist between calls. We observe
the returned row, or termination when the ant leaves another side, but not elapsed
time or which terminal side is used.
\begin{theorem}[Boundary-response threshold]\label{five:thm:index}
For each fixed entry side and parity sector, this interface has respectively
$1,2,7,269$ response types at heights $1,2,3,4$, over all finite widths and all
colorings. At every height $h\ge5$ its index is infinite. More precisely, at
width $2m+4$ there are $m+1$ pairwise distinguishable five-row colorings, even
when the only experiment is repetition of a single input row.
\end{theorem}

The height-four catalogue was established above; the same finite closure
check gives the smaller-height cases. The construction below establishes
the infinite-index side and its autonomous composition.

\subsection{Complete column states and local transformations}
\subsubsection{Conventions}
Rows are $y=0,1,2,3,4$. Headings north, east, south, west are $0,1,2,3$.
Time $t$ is observed before update $t$. A column mask is
$b=\sum_{y=0}^4 b_y2^y$, with $b_y=1$ for black. A word of masks denotes all
colors in consecutive columns, left to right. Products concatenate; powers
are ordinary finite repetitions, not symmetric differences.

Define four fixed complete column words
\begin{equation}\label{five:eq:blocks}
 A=(6,5),\qquad B=(10,9),\qquad
 C=(6,10,12,17),\qquad V=(18,29),
\end{equation}
and, for $j,k\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{five:eq:counter}
 \R(j,k)=A^jCV^k,\qquad \Lft(j,k)=(5)\R(j,k).
\end{equation}
The respective entry poses are $(0,3),\E$ and $(0,2),\E$.
Every cell outside a displayed block is white unless part of an explicitly
specified neighboring block. An excursion ends as soon as its head leaves the
block, before reading an exterior cell.

\subsection{A unary counter with a persistent complete residual}
\begin{lemma}[Counter recurrence]\label{five:lem:counter}
For $k>0$, an excursion in $\R(j,k)$ from $(0,3),\E$ returns at $(-1,2),\W$
after $8j+30$ updates and leaves exactly $\R(j+1,k-1)$. For $k=0$, it exits
at $(2j+4,1),\E$ after $4j+16$ updates, leaving $B^{j+1}(10,18)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Apply the eastward $A\to B$ kernel $j$ times. The prefix $A^j$ becomes $B^j$
and the head reaches the first column of $C$, at row 3 facing east, after $4j$
updates. No later block has yet been read.

When $k>0$, apply the $CV\to AC$ kernel. Its final position is the preceding
column at row 2 facing west. It then traverses $B^j$ backwards, using the
$B\to A$ kernel $j$ times. The prefix is restored, the shifted cap is $AC$,
and the untouched suffix is $V^{k-1}$. Thus the entire block is
$A^{j+1}CV^{k-1}$ and the duration is $4j+30+4j$. All stages read only their
stated subblocks; a final move into a neighboring block does not update it.

When $k=0$, the terminal $C$ kernel exits right. There is no return scan, so
$B^j$ remains and the complete output is $B^{j+1}(10,18)$. The duration is
$4j+16$. None of the kernels exits through a horizontal boundary.
\end{proof}

Consequently, repeated row-3 requests produce $k$ row-2 replies followed by a
terminal reply on request $k+1$. The residual after $q$ successes is exactly
$\R(j+q,k-q)$, not a reset or a retained core with unspecified debris.

\begin{lemma}[Port adjustment]\label{five:lem:port}
A successful request to $\Lft(j,k)$ from $(0,2),\E$, for $k>0$, returns at
$(-1,3),\W$, takes $8j+34$ updates, and leaves $\Lft(j+1,k-1)$. At $k=0$
it exits far at $(2j+5,1),\E$ in $4j+18$ updates, leaving
$(9)B^{j+1}(10,18)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The leading column executes $5\to9$ on entry, supplying the row-3 entry for
Lemma~\ref{five:lem:counter}. A successful return supplies row 2 facing west to the
$9\to5$ kernel, restoring the leading column and returning at row 3. At
exhaustion there is no return through that column, so its mask remains 9.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Distinguishable histories at a common width]\label{five:prop:distinguish}
For fixed $m\ge0$, the complete colorings $\R(m-k,k)$, $0\le k\le m$, all have
width $2m+4$ and have pairwise distinct time-blind same-side response types.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For two remaining counts $k<k'$, issue $k+1$ copies of the row-3 request. The
first block terminates on the last request while the second still returns
row 2. Both share the same physical width; neither time nor interior colors
are observed. Adding white rows above row 4 does not change any read, and
termination is through the far vertical boundary. The construction therefore
works at every height at least five. Lemma~\ref{five:lem:port} covers the other
entry parity, and rotation covers the opposite side.
\end{proof}
An exact summary capable of answering these experiments at width $2m+4$ must
therefore have at least $m+1$ values, or at least $\lceil\log_2(m+1)\rceil$
bits. This is a memory lower bound for the specified response observable as the
finite source width grows.

\subsection{Autonomous composition and the exact quadratic exit}
An interface request is an intervention: the experimenter supplies the next
entry. We now remove that intervention entirely.

Let $\J$ reflect a five-row block in the line $x=-1/2$ and complement every
color inside that block. Reflection alone exchanges left and right turns;
color complementation exchanges their conditions, restoring the original
rule. Thus $\J$ is an exact conjugacy on every update inside the block. This
is only a local transformation up to exit: the infinite exterior is not
complemented and the constructed black set remains finite.

For $l,r\ge0$, place $\R(0,r)$ in columns $0,\ldots,2r+3$, and the reflected,
complemented $\Lft(0,l)$ in columns $-2l-5,\ldots,-1$. Start at $(0,3),\E$.
In an ordinary left-to-right list the complete input is
\begin{equation}\label{five:eq:source}
 \operatorname{rev}\bigl(31-((5)CV^l)\bigr)\;CV^r,
\end{equation}
where $31-$ acts entrywise. The rectangle has width $2(l+r)+9$ and exactly
$6r+4l+23$ black cells. The rest of the plane is white.

A right return has pose $(-1,2),\W$, precisely the entry for the reflected
left block. A left return has pose $(0,3),\E$, precisely the right entry.
No pointer, phase, color, or environment is reset between requests.

\begin{lemma}[Complete round induction]\label{five:lem:round}
After $j$ complete right/left pairs, for $0\le j\le\min(l,r)$, the head is
$(0,3),\E$ and the full two-sided field is
\begin{equation}\label{five:eq:checkpoint}
 \J\bigl[(5)\R(j,l-j)\bigr]\quad\big|\quad\R(j,r-j).
\end{equation}
The time is $t_j=8j^2+56j$. No prior update has left the original rectangle.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The claim at zero is the definition. If both counters are positive, the next
right excursion takes $8j+30$ steps and the next left excursion takes $8j+34$,
by Lemmas~\ref{five:lem:counter} and~\ref{five:lem:port} with the local conjugacy.
Their complete outputs give~\eqref{five:eq:checkpoint} at $j+1$. Their kernel
footprints exclude premature vertical or far exits. Summing $16j+64$ over
$j=0,\ldots,q-1$ gives $8q^2+56q$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Two-parameter complete exit]\label{five:thm:asym}
The source~\eqref{five:eq:source} first exits at
\[
T(l,r)=
\begin{cases}
8r^2+60r+16,&r\le l,\\
8l^2+68l+48,&l<r.
\end{cases}
\]
For $r\le l$, the exit pose is $(2r+4,1),\E$, and the full endpoint is
\[
 \J[(5)\R(r,l-r)]\quad\big|\quad B^{r+1}(10,18).
\]
For $l<r$, the exit pose is $(-2l-6,1),\W$, and the full endpoint is
\[
 \J[(9)B^{l+1}(10,18)]\quad\big|\quad\R(l+1,r-l-1).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If $r\le l$, execute $r$ full pairs and then the right terminal call. Its
$4r+16$ additional updates give the first formula and the full endpoint.
If $l<r$, execute $l$ pairs, one successful right call costing $8l+30$, and a
terminal left call costing $4l+18$. These give the second formula and endpoint.
The footprint guards prove this is the first exit, not only an observed exit.
\end{proof}

Taking $l=r=k$ proves Theorem~\ref{five:thm:quadratic}. Each completed excursion
crosses the seam once; the terminal right excursion does not cross it, so
there are $2k$ seam crossings. The pair-checkpoint black mass stays
$6r+4l+23$: the right block loses two black cells per pair while the complemented
left block gains two. Equality of black mass and pose does not mean equality
of complete states; the cap locations and rewritten columns change.

For arbitrary $n\ge9$, use $k=\lfloor(n-9)/4\rfloor$ and add white columns to
the observation rectangle. Enlarging a region cannot decrease its first-exit
time. White rows added above likewise preserve the lower bound. Hence no
finite-valued $f(h)$ can bound $S(h,n)$ by $f(h)n$ for all $h,n$. In particular,
the open linear-bound question of~\cite{etse} already has a negative answer at
height five. The known $O(n^3)$ upper bound there leaves a gap; we do not
claim optimality of the quadratic coefficient or of the constructed family.

\subsection{The finite/infinite response boundary}
To complete Theorem~\ref{five:thm:index}, we specify the finite closure being used
on the other side of the threshold. The invariant
$\epsilon=(h-x-y)\bmod2$ fixes the horizontal entry rows of a column.
Writing $p=(x+\epsilon)\bmod2$, entry rows have parity $1-p$ and output rows
have parity $p$.

A finite persistent response type is a tuple with one branch for each allowed
input row. Each branch is terminal or consists of an output channel and a
residual type. Define its rank as the maximal number of successful returns
along any input sequence. Colors persist through all these calls. Terminal
side and time are deliberately unobserved, so this quotient does not by itself
encode the exact time calculations in the preceding sections.

\subsubsection{Finite certificate and its interpretation}
The finite catalogs are
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}rrrr@{}}\toprule
Height & Types per side/parity & Maximal rank & Closure width\\\midrule
1&1&0&1\\2&2&1&2\\3&7&3&3\\4&269&13&8\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The fourth row is the persistent response classification of~Proposition~\ref{prop:catalogue}.
The supplementary checker independently rebuilds all four rows. For height
four the exact-width type sets, at widths zero through eight, have sizes
\[1,\ 12,\ 36,\ 83,\ 158,\ 240,\ 264,\ 269,\ 269.\]
Importantly, the last two \emph{sets}, not merely their cardinalities, coincide.

Adjoin any possible near-column mask to any opposite-parity child type.
A horizontal one-column excursion makes at most two updates, then exits that
column or the strip. If it enters the child, use the child's declared response,
retain its residual type and resume with the modified near-column mask.
Every successful child reply decreases child rank. A near-side parent reply
must match the declared residual product and decrease parent rank.

The certificate checks these local equations for every mask, child type and
input channel. Induction on width proves their physical interpretation:
assuming the complete child has the specified persistent behavior, the exact
one-column transitions and its retained replies uniquely determine the parent.
Child rank ensures each request terminates; parent rank controls repeated
requests. The empty block starts the induction. Every catalog type has a
concrete representative, whose full response tree is also replayed.

Finally, distinct finite labeled response trees have a distinguishing finite
input word: select a branch on which they differ, or recurse to distinct residuals.
Thus the catalog is an exact quotient for the stated observable, not only an
overapproximation. Proposition~\ref{five:prop:distinguish} proves infinite index
starting at five rows and completes Theorem~\ref{five:thm:index}.

\subsection{Exact macro-dynamics beyond semilinear output}
The counter construction admits a compact macro-description: the processed
length $j$, remaining lengths $l-j,r-j$, and current side specify the complete
column words and pose. Counter updates and the duration of one call are affine.
There are, however, an input-dependent number of calls, and their sum is quadratic.

\begin{corollary}\label{five:cor:nonsemi}
There is a Presburger-definable finite-fiber family of complete initial ant
states whose exact first-exit-time graph is not semilinear.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
In~\eqref{five:eq:source} with $l=r=k$, black membership is specified by linear
interval endpoints, parity and fixed row masks. Hence the source family and
its observation rectangle are Presburger-definable. Its time graph is
$\{(k,8k^2+60k+16):k\ge0\}$.

If this graph were a finite union of linear sets, some infinite component
would contain $(k_0+ma,t_0+mb)$ for all $m\ge0$, with nonzero period $(a,b)$.
A graph of a function cannot have $a=0,b\ne0$; nonnegativity forces $a>0$.
The quadratic identity then has a nonzero $m^2$ coefficient $8a^2$, contradicting
the affine second coordinate. Thus the graph is not semilinear.
\end{proof}

Thus a finite-parameter macro-description can give a nonsemilinear accumulated
time graph. The displayed quadratic remains directly evaluable in polynomial
bit time, a distinction used in the complete-state algorithm below.
