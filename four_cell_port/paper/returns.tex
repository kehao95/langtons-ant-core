\section{Composable excursions with genuine old-wake
readback}\label{composable-excursions-with-genuine-old-wake-readback}

The preceding rules keep the highway advancing east. Here we construct an
excursion that turns northwest, reverses southeast, and reads a black cell
written during its earlier approach. A final marker restores a northeast
highway. Three markers specify one such excursion; any finite number of these
three-marker patterns can be installed in the initial field.

Theorem~\ref{th:return-train} proves arbitrary finite composition, with an
independent excursion parameter $n_i\ge100$ and incoming gap $m_i\ge100$
for each pattern. The five stages of one pattern are shown below. Their
purpose is to establish a complete field identity at the old-cell read,
independent of how far the middle flight travels.

The coordinates $s=x+y$, $\ell=y-x$ and $r=x-y$ describe the three guards.
Northeast motion increases $s$ by four per period. Northwest motion holds
$s$ fixed and increases $\ell$; southeast motion holds $s$ fixed and
increases $r$. These directions isolate the distant reversal and then the
return region from the fields left by earlier stages.

\subsection{Convention, standard word, and finite mature
restrictions}\label{returns-2}

Use the state and clockwise-rotation conventions of Section~1 and the integer
heading convention $\mathrm N,\mathrm E,\mathrm S,\mathrm W=0,1,2,3$ of
Section~\ref{scattering-2}. All
fields in this section are complete finite black sets.

Use exactly the standard word w and thirteen-cell NE seed K from
\path{four_cell_port/verification/guarded_scattering/check.py}. Let
$P_i,H_i$ be its 104 pre-update positions and headings, with $P_0=0$,
$H_0=\mathrm E$ and $d=(2,2)$. Its minimum future value of x+y is zero. Write $w_a$
for its cyclic word beginning at phase a, and $R_k$ for clockwise
rotation through $k$ quarter-turns, with $k=0,1,2,3$ giving NE, SE, SW, NW
drift respectively. Step $i$ of $w_a$ has canonical phase $(a+i)\bmod104$;
the subscripts of $Q_{B,i}$ and $Q_{D,i}(0)$ below index these relative steps.
These phase coordinates specify the
reference trajectories used in each stage of the construction.

Put

\[
s(x,y)=x+y,\quad r(x,y)=x-y,\quad\ell(x,y)=y-x,\quad G=-160.
\]

For a phase j, orientation k, and a linear form f with $f(R_kd)>0$, define

\begin{equation}\label{eq:rt21}
\begin{aligned}M(j,k,f,G)&=\mathop{\triangle}_{i=0}^{103}\ \mathop{\triangle}_{L_i\le a\le u_i}\{R_k(P_i-P_j)+aR_kd\},\\L_i&=\left\lceil\frac{G-f(R_k(P_i-P_j))}{f(R_kd)}\right\rceil,\\u_i&=\begin{cases}0&i<j,\\-1&i\ge j.\end{cases}\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

This is finite. It is the exact parity of all planned standard writes
before a phase-j collision, restricted to $f\ge G$. It is a
description of a finite restriction, not an infinite physical input. A
finite approach realizes it once the old physical endpoint and the
synthetic past omitted before that approach lie outside the comparison
half-plane. Those obligations are proved below, not assumed from
observed repetition.

Define the marker coordinates
\[
q_2(n)=(-2n-15,2n+11),\qquad q_3=(177,-260).
\]
Our first collision interface has head 0, heading $H_{57}=\mathrm S$ and field

\begin{equation}\label{eq:rt22}
\begin{gathered}M_1\cup\{0,q_2(n),q_3\}\cup H,\\M_1=M(57,0,s,G),\qquad H\subset\{s<G\}.\end{gathered}
\end{equation}

where H is any finite complete historical black field. The two-marker
version omits $q_3$. Future packets add further markers above the current
packet's entire read extent; their noninterference is proved in
Subsection \ref{returns-8}.

\subsection{Finite A and C blocks, and the first long
flight}\label{returns-3}

\subsubsection{First turn A}\label{first-turn-a}

The exact 832-update orbit from $(M_1\cup\{0\},0,H_{57})$ ends at

\[
v_A=(-12,12),\qquad h_A=\mathrm S,
\]

with 580 black cells and a certified standard NW tail $w_{58}$. Every A
read has $-10\le s\le9$ and every tail read has
$s\ge G$. Both infinite checkers accept this complete reference
endpoint. Its initial field has 484 cells.

Let $Q_{B,i}$ be the pre-update positions in the primary collision frame of
the flight $w_{58}$ launched from $v_A$ at $n=0$. Thus $Q_{B,0}=v_A=(-12,12)$
and $Q_{B,14}=(-15,11)$, so

\[
q_2(n)=Q_{B,14}+n(-2,2)=(-2n-15,2n+11),
\]

and the first reference read there is exactly $104n+14$ updates later. The
finite ray calculation enumerates every i and integer z with
$q_2(0)-Q_{B,i}=z(-2,2)$. All n+z are nonnegative for $n\ge100$,
and $\min(104z+i)=14$. The expected letter is R. This checks first contact,
not just that the proposed marker eventually lies on the reference
route.

The marker $q_2(n)$ is strictly left of all A reads, for every
$n\ge100$. The fixed $q_3$ is absent from A and from every future
NW phase ray. The old field H is outside $s\ge G$; the A prefix
and its NW reference remain inside. First-difference coupling proves the
actual A block and the subsequent $104n+14$-update flight B, ending at the
black marker $q_2$. Its absolute packet time is $104n+846$.

\subsubsection{Distant reversal C}\label{distant-reversal-c}

The collision has canonical phase $58+14=72$ and rotation $k=3$.
Let $M_2=M(72,3,\ell,G)$. The exact 51272-update orbit from

\[
(M_2\cup\{0\},0,(H_{72}+3)\bmod4)
\]

ends at $v_C=(-24,-44)$, heading N, with 2569 black cells and a certified
SE reference tail $w_{18}$. Its initial field has 485 black cells. All its
finite reads have $\ell\ge G$ and, after translation by $q_2(n)$,
$s\ge G$. Its bounds are $-75\le s\le63$ and
$-76\le r\le54$ in local coordinates.

The true field before this C block agrees with this kernel on

\begin{equation}\label{eq:rt31}
\Omega_C(n)=\{v:s(v)\ge G,\ \ell(v-q_2(n))\ge G\}.
\end{equation}

Indeed every black cell of the A endpoint and $q_3$ lies strictly below the
ell boundary. So does every point of the synthetic standard past
preceding the A endpoint. Their maxima are finite; subtracting
$\ell(q_2(n))=4n+26$ makes each required strict inequality an integer affine
inequality in n.~They hold at n=100 with nonnegative slope thereafter.
Hence inside \eqref{eq:rt31}, the actual B writes equal exactly the mature $M_2$
writes from \eqref{eq:rt21}. The marker adds \{0\}. The arbitrary H contributes
nothing because of the s boundary.

Thus C is an actual literal block on the complete input. No all-future
conclusion for the true orbit is inferred from its isolated-kernel tail:
the older field outside $\Omega_C$ will matter during the reverse flight.

\subsection{The first return to a previously written black cell}\label{returns-4}

At C's end the head is

\begin{equation}\label{eq:rt41}
q_2(n)+v_C=(-2n-39,2n-33),\qquad h=\mathrm N.
\end{equation}

at packet time $104n+52118$. Its isolated reference follows $w_{18}$
southeast. Let $Q_{D,i}(0)$ be the 104 phase positions from (-39,-33),
heading N. For general n the phase positions are $Q_{D,i}(0)-n(2,-2)$.

The true field and this reference differ by the following exact XOR:

\begin{equation}\label{eq:rt42}
B_{A,\rm out}\mathbin{\triangle}B_{\rm writes}\mathbin{\triangle}(q_2(n)+M_2)\mathbin{\triangle}\{q_3\}\mathbin{\triangle}H.
\end{equation}
Here $B_{A,\rm out}$ is the complete field after block $A$, and
$B_{\rm writes}$ is the symmetric difference of all read sites during flight
$B$, counting each visit once.


The finite C writes cancel because both trajectories execute them
identically. The two copies of the marker $q_2$ also cancel. This identity
retains every older write; it does not identify the true state with a
cleaned reference.

All reverse reference phase rays have s values strictly below both the
NW B write stream and $q_2(n)+M_2$, while still having $s\ge G$.
Therefore they cannot read those two terms or H. The remaining
first-difference query is against the fixed finite A endpoint and $q_3$.

For each phase $i$ and black point $b\in B_{A,\mathrm{out}}\cup\{q_3\}$,
enumerate integers $z$ satisfying

\[
b-Q_{D,i}(0)=z(2,-2).
\]

There are 158 phase/cell candidates. Every n+z is nonnegative for
$n\ge100$. The minimum of 104z+i is -170, attained at phase
index 38 and $b=q^*=(-38,-32)$. No candidate belongs to $q_3$. The reference
letter there is R and the actual cell is black. Hence the actual reverse
flight has exact length

\begin{equation}\label{eq:rt43}
104n-170=104(n-2)+38.
\end{equation}

and ends in the old black read at packet time $208n+51948$. Before it, all
turns agree with the SE reference. At it, the real turn is L rather than
R.

\subsubsection{The black cell was created by the same actual
trajectory}\label{the-black-cell-was-created-by-the-same-actual-trajectory}

In the incoming NE plan relative to phase 57, solve

The last such read is $i=89,\ a=-17$, at time -1736, and $w_{89}=R$. The A

pre-input lifetime, or a cell that is simply presumed eventually
relevant.

\subsection{A constant full comparison field after an arbitrarily long
excursion}\label{returns-5}

Use the fixed region

\begin{equation}\label{eq:rt51}
\Omega=\{s\ge G,\ r\ge G\}.
\end{equation}

At the old read $q^*$, the true field restricted to $\Omega$ is independent of
n. This is the closure-enabling fact. The arbitrary H is absent by its s
guard. The distant $q_2$ marker and all C reads are outside $\Omega$ for every
$n\ge100$: their r values are -4n-26 plus a bounded local value,
strictly below G. This does not say they were erased; they remain in the
complete true endpoint.

For completeness, here is an explicit finite XOR formula for the field
in $\Omega$. Start with $B_{A,\mathrm{out}}\cup\{q_3\}$. For each $Q_{B,i}$ put

\[
h_i=\left\lfloor\frac{r(Q_{B,i})-G}{4}\right\rfloor.
\]

Add by XOR all $Q_{B,i}+j(-2,2)$ with $0\le j\le h_i$ and
$s(Q_{B,i})\ge G$. All $h_i<100$ (in fact $32\le h_i\le35$ and $\sum_i(h_i+1)=3618$),
so all epochs $0\le j\le h_i$ are present for every $n\ge100$. The terminal 14 B reads at epoch
n are outside $\Omega$. Thus these are exactly the B writes inside $\Omega$,
not a truncation by simulation time.

For the reverse flight write its complete epochs as v=j-n, so
$-n\le v\le-3$. For each $Q_{D,i}(0)$ put

\[
l_i=\left\lceil\frac{G-r(Q_{D,i}(0))}{4}\right\rceil.
\]

Add by XOR $Q_{D,i}(0)+v(2,-2)$ for $l_i\le v\le-3$ when
$s(Q_{D,i}(0))\ge G$. All $l_i\ge-n$ for
$n\ge100$. Finally add the first 38 phase positions at v=-2, if
they lie in $\Omega$. Restrict the resulting finite field to $\Omega$.

The resulting field $C_{\rm old}$ has 1446 cells. Its exact hash and the 3618 B
terms and 3893 D terms are recorded in
\path{four_cell_port/verification/return_scattering/report.json},
reconstructed by the adjacent \texttt{check.py} in the pinned verification snapshot. This is equality of
the entire inside field, including the old A scar and both relevant
write streams. It is not equality on the next few read positions or a
bounded mask inferred from matching poses. Every lower/upper epoch
omission is justified by the linear forms above.

\subsection{Single-packet theorem and protection under later
interruptions}\label{returns-6}

\begin{theorem}[One return pattern]\label{th:return-one} Every state with initial field \eqref{eq:rt22}, initial pose
$(0,0),\mathrm S$, $n\ge100$ and arbitrary finite $H\subset\{s<G\}$, has the three-marker
behavior just described and reaches NE standard phase 94 by time
$208n+75609$. The two-marker state has the SE output from Subsection~\ref{two-marker-output}. All supplied black history remains physically
present.
\end{theorem}

\textbf{Proof.} Subsections \ref{returns-3} and \ref{returns-4} give the exact A, B, C, D execution and
first old read. Subsection \ref{returns-5} gives the exact whole-field
restriction and protected finite E continuation and tail. The comparison
paths remain within the regions on which field agreement was
established, so a first divergent read is impossible. This is a
finite-stage induction plus an infinite-tail certificate, not
extrapolation from the sampled n values. \(\square\)

For the three-marker packet, every read before \eqref{eq:rt52} satisfies

\begin{equation}\label{eq:rt61}
-109\le s\le59.
\end{equation}

The upper bound also covers all initial $M_1$ cells and the three markers.
The NW and SE flights have constant s on each phase ray; their
arbitrarily long lengths do not enlarge \eqref{eq:rt61}. The finite A, C, E blocks
supply the other extrema. At the C boundary, \eqref{eq:rt41} gives x=-2n-39, so no
n-independent backward x-displacement or bounded-diameter packet
footprint can replace this argument.

A later marker may interrupt the final NE tail. The single-packet
theorem alone would not guarantee old-history protection after that
interruption. The next section supplies the necessary nested-guard
induction for the full composition.

\subsection{Finite arithmetic connector and marker
ordering}\label{returns-7}

A packet outputs pose $v_{\rm out}$, orientation NE, phase 94. After
$m\ge100$ full periods and phase adjustment to 57, its next
primary marker is displaced by

\begin{equation}\label{eq:rt71}
\Delta(m)=v_{\rm out}+(m+1)d+P_{57}-P_{94}=(2m+208,2m-242).
\end{equation}

Thus \(s(\Delta(m))=4m-34\ge366\). The next diagonal guard starts
at \(s(\Delta(m))+G\ge206\), while all previous packet black cells
and reads not in inherited history have $s\le59$. The minimum guard
slack is 147, strictly positive. Every synthetic pre-output standard
write is also below the next boundary; this is checked for each phase at
its latest possible pre-output epoch, with all earlier epochs decreasing
s further.

It follows that, at the next primary, the full inside field consists
exactly of its $M_1$, its three still-unread markers, and still later
preinstalled markers. The actual NE flight writes and the mature
synthetic writes agree there because the omitted pre-flight past is
entirely outside. Inherited earlier history is below an even older guard
and hence below the new one.

The companion markers of this next packet cannot interrupt that NE
approach: $q_2(n)$ and $q_3$ have r respectively -4n-26 and 437 relative to
the primary, outside all two-sided incoming NE phase lines. This is a
geometric disjointness test, not merely checking a finite flight. Nor
can markers from a still later packet cause a premature contact. Their
minimum relative s is -83; \eqref{eq:rt71} places them strictly above both the
current packet's entire read maximum and the maximum s of an incoming
flight before its primary. The latter is at most primary s+1, as checked
from the 104 phases with their latest pre-collision epochs.

These inequalities prove the chronological order of all planned
contacts, not just compatibility of consecutive endpoint poses. For
$m>100$ their left sides increase by $4(m-100)$; no further
trajectory simulation is required.

\subsection{Arbitrarily many genuine returns from one complete finite
input}\label{returns-8}

Choose any integer $k\ge0$ and any integer lists

\[
m_1,\ldots,m_k\ge100,\qquad n_1,\ldots,n_k\ge100.
\]

Start with pose $p_0=0$, phase $a_0=0$, orientation NE. For i=1,\ldots,k
set

\begin{equation}\label{eq:rt81}
\begin{aligned}Q_i&=p_{i-1}+(m_i+\mathbf1_{\{57<a_{i-1}\}})d+P_{57}-P_{a_{i-1}},\\p_i&=Q_i+v_{\rm out},\qquad a_i=94.\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

Install at input time all three cells

\[
Q_i,\qquad Q_i+q_2(n_i),\qquad Q_i+q_3.
\]

The complete input is K plus these cells plus any finite black $G_0\subset\{s<0\}$; its ant starts at 0,E. All these sets are disjoint, and
the input has exactly $13+3k+|G_0|$ black cells. Markers
are never installed during execution. For k=0 the initial state is
already on the certified NE future.

\begin{theorem}[Arbitrary finite return trains]\label{th:return-train} Every such finite
input follows the prescribed packets with all past writes retained and
reaches permanent standard P104 by

\begin{equation}\label{eq:rt82}
T=\sum_{i=1}^k\bigl[104m_i+((57-a_{i-1})\bmod104)+208n_i+75609\bigr].
\end{equation}

The output after $k>0$ packets is $p_k$, heading E, NE phase 94.
Each packet contains the genuine post-input-created black lifetime
\eqref{eq:rt44}.
\end{theorem}

\textbf{Proof.} The canonical initial ray has $s\ge0$, so it
cannot read $G_0$. The first primary guard is beyond every K cell and the
entire synthetic pre-start past; at $m_1=100$ its slack is at least 243.
Its companion markers miss the incoming NE rail, and later markers are
beyond the finite read envelope. Therefore the first collision has the
correct complete restricted field \eqref{eq:rt22}.

Apply Theorem~\ref{th:return-one} up to its output, but keep all later markers in the
physical state. Subsection \ref{returns-7} proves that they have not
altered this prefix, that the next collision has exactly the required
restricted field, and that its guard is strictly above all preceding
history. Induct on i. The guards strictly increase in the same absolute
linear form s. Between collisions the previous certified tail remains
above its previous guard; within each new packet all reads stay above
the new guard. Consequently no later reset reactivates a retired region.
After the last packet there is no remaining forward perturbation and the
certified NE tail persists. Summing the exact block lengths proves
\eqref{eq:rt82}. \(\square\)

The assertion about arbitrary finite $G_0$ is a proved initial guard, not a
deletion of an unknown future-active set. Complete-state train replays
retain $G_0$ and every black point from every earlier excursion.
