\begin{abstract}
We prove a computer-assisted rigidity theorem for finite-support periodic
highways of the classical two-colour Langton ant. Suppose a heading-restoring
period has full drift $(2,-2)$, net black growth twelve, and transverse width
ten. If each strictly interior transverse level contains at most one odd
translation class, then the period is $104$ and its read word is the standard
highway. Neither the complete wake, the position of its missing interior
strand, nor a period bound is prescribed. An analytical parity argument forces
both boundary residues to be occupied and reduces the transverse profile to
five possibilities. A residue-independent integer certificate supplies period
bounds between $160$ and $192$. Exact modular edge counts reduce the remaining
choices to $76$ normalized wake assignments. Complete ordered-event searches
leave only the standard trace. The classification derives the standard layer profile and its longitudinal
residues from these sparse geometric data. General wake-dependent period
bounds and a terminating inverse-synthesis algorithm for every non-axis drift
are retained as a second part of the theory. A quadratic wake character extends this parity method to every non-axis
full drift, is computable in polynomial bit time, and excludes an explicit
growth-eight family of unbounded span. A forced-layer argument also gives
a purely analytic exclusion of unit-diagonal full drift.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords.} Langton's ant; finite support; periodic highway;
computer-assisted proof; wake rigidity; exact enumeration.

\section{Introduction and main theorem}
The classical Langton ant is a deterministic walker on a two-coloured square
lattice. On white it turns right, on black it turns left; it then toggles the
current colour and moves one lattice edge. The finite-support Highway
Conjecture asserts that every finite initial black set eventually produces
the standard period-$104$ translating trace. This statement includes both an
\emph{entrance problem}, concerning eventual periodicity, and a
\emph{classification problem}, concerning the possible periodic limits.
The present paper advances the second problem.

A periodic highway emits an unbounded black wake, even though its seed is
finite. The wake can be specified by the translation classes receiving an odd
number of visits in one period. A previous exact classification fixed all
these classes to those of the standard highway~\cite{KeStandard}. Here the
classes themselves are unknown. We retain only a small set of numerical and
sparsity hypotheses and derive the complete standard wake as a conclusion.

\begin{definition}
For a heading-restoring word $w=w_0\cdots w_{P-1}$, let $q_i\in\Z^2$ be its
pre-update positions and $d=q_P-q_0$ its drift. For $d=(2,-2)$ the full classes
modulo $\Z d$ have labels
\[
(t,r)=(x+y,x\bmod2).
\]
Let $o_t$ be the number of classes at level $t$ visited an odd number of times
in the period. Let $g=\#\Rr-\#\Ll$ and
\[
W=\max_i(q_{i,x}+q_{i,y})-\min_i(q_{i,x}+q_{i,y}).
\]
The wake is \emph{interior-thin} when $o_t\le1$ at each level strictly between
the two extrema.
\end{definition}

The word ``thin'' refers to odd-class occupancy, not to the number of visits
or crossings. There may be many visits on an interior level. Nor does it say
that an ordinary row of the infinite colouring contains at most one black cell.

\begin{theorem}[Thin-wake rigidity]\label{thm:main}
Let a word $w$ repeat forever under the classical Langton rule from a complete
finite black seed. Suppose it restores the heading, has full drift $(2,-2)$,
has net black growth $g=12$, and has transverse width $W=10$. If its wake is
interior-thin, then $P=104$ and $w$ is a cyclic phase of the standard highway.
\end{theorem}

There is no period bound in this hypothesis. The seed need not occupy a
prescribed box, and genuinely unread stationary debris is allowed. The theorem
also applies to an eventual periodic suffix after an arbitrary finite transient.
Its boundary state is again a complete finite-support state.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:thick}
Any nonstandard finite-support periodic highway with drift $(2,-2)$, growth
twelve, and transverse width ten has both longitudinal residue classes odd
on at least one strictly interior transverse level.
\end{corollary}

These numerical and structural hypotheses are not asserted for every finite
seed. In particular, Theorem~\ref{thm:main} does not solve the entrance problem
or classify other drifts, growths, widths or thicker wakes. Repeating the
standard period twice changes the drift to $(4,-4)$ and growth to $24$; it is
not a second period in the fixed-drift problem.

Jillhewar's transverse exclusions already give width at least ten for every
diagonal finite-support periodic highway~\cite[Section~9]{Jillhewar2026}.
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} addresses uniqueness within that smallest surviving
width, with the additional full-drift, growth and interior-thinness hypotheses.
The general inverse problem in Appendix~\ref{app:general} starts instead from
a prescribed wake and non-axis drift and computes a terminating search range.

The proof combines analytical reductions with finite integer computations.
The supplied-word realization criterion is prior work~\cite{Jillhewar2026};
short-factor constraints belong to the symbolic study of the ant~\cite{Gajardo2003}.
The specific upper certificate and ordered-event engine are retained from the
standard-wake project~\cite{KeStandard}. The contribution here is the removal
of the complete-wake and missing-level hypotheses, together with the complete
classification of the resulting finite family. No lower-width exclusion is
used as a premise. Appendix~\ref{app:mixed} develops a separate
necessary parity test for arbitrary-width mixed wakes, extending the attributed
port-permutation factorization.

\section{Finite support and stabilized visit order}
A complete state is $X=(B,p,h)$, where $B\subset\Z^2$ is finite and
$h\in\{N,E,S,W\}$. Write $F$ for one literal update and
$\chi(x,y)=(-1)^{x+y}$. The quantity
\[
\sigma(X)=\begin{cases}\chi(p),&h=N,S,\\-\chi(p),&h=E,W\end{cases}
\]
is invariant: each update changes both position parity and heading axis.
All times below are pre-update times.

Fix a nonzero drift and write the positions in one class as $q_i=b+a_i d$.
A visit to the physical cell $b+n d$ at phase $i$ occurs, when present, at time
\[
(n-a_i)P+i.
\]
Thus the stabilized order of phases in that class is decreasing $a_i$, followed
by increasing $i$. Equivalently, its keys are $i-Pa_i$.

\begin{lemma}[Supplied-word criterion]\label{lem:criterion}
A heading-restoring nonzero-drift word admits a finite-support realization if
and only if every class, in stabilized order, alternates
$\Rr,\Ll,\Rr,\Ll,\ldots$ starting with $\Rr$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Far enough along a class every phase is present, and infinitely many such
physical cells are initially white. Their first turn is $\Rr$; successive
reads toggle the colour. This proves necessity.
For sufficiency, the phases available at $b+n d$ form the suffix obtained by
removing all phases with $a_i>n$. For
$\min a_i\le n<\max a_i$, initially blacken this cell precisely when the
number of removed phases is odd; leave every other cell white. Each available
suffix starts with the correct colour and alternates. Induction in real time
verifies every prescribed update. Only finitely many boundary cells have been
blackened. This is the known construction in~\cite{Jillhewar2026}.
\end{proof}

Each class therefore contributes zero or one to $\#\Rr-\#\Ll$, according as
its cardinality is even or odd. In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:growth}g=\sum_t o_t.\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Extreme classes]\label{lem:rails}
Every visited class on a transverse extreme has exactly one event, a right
turn. In the top-rail normalization the top is $t=0$, the head faces east,
$\sigma=-1$, and the bottom is $t=-10$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
At an extreme, one neighbour on each coordinate axis is outside the trace.
The invariant $\sigma$ fixes the arrival axis. Hence every visit to a fixed
extreme class has the same arrival and departure directions, and thus the
same turn. Lemma~\ref{lem:criterion} forces this constant stabilized word to
be the singleton $\Rr$. At the top the only compatible right-turning pose is
east-facing; at the bottom it is west-facing. Translate a top event to the
origin. The stated gauge and width follow.
\end{proof}

There are at most two extreme classes on either boundary, because the quotient
is by the full vector $(2,-2)$, not its primitive direction. The geometric
interval is a consequence of the candidate's stated width and normalization;
it is not a wall added to the original dynamics.

\section{Five possible profiles, derived analytically}
Let $m_t$ count the phase events on level $t$. Let $u_t$ count straight passages
from $t-1$ through $t$ to $t+1$. Flow balance in the closed transverse walk gives
the same number of reverse straight passages. At even $t$, right turns reverse
the transverse direction and left turns continue it; at odd $t$ the roles
are reversed. Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:row}
m_t=(-1)^t o_t+4u_t.
\end{equation}
The movement axis alternates, so there are $P/2$ horizontal moves. Their net
displacement is two, implying that $P/2$ is even. Summing~\eqref{eq:row} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:parity}
P\equiv0\pmod4,\qquad \sum_t(-1)^t o_t\equiv0\pmod4.
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Profile reduction]\label{prop:profiles}
Under Theorem~\ref{thm:main}'s hypotheses both extreme levels have two odd
classes. Exactly one interior level is empty, and it is one of
\[
h\in\{-9,-7,-5,-3,-1\}.
\]
All other interior levels have one odd class.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The two rails supply at most four odd classes; the nine interior levels supply
at most nine. Growth twelve forces the rail total to be three or four. If it
is three, all nine interior levels are occupied. Their signed sum is $4-5=-1$,
so the total in~\eqref{eq:parity} is two, a contradiction.
Thus the rail total is four and exactly eight interior levels are occupied.
If $h$ is the empty level, the signed sum is
$4+(4-5)-(-1)^h=3-(-1)^h$. It is divisible by four exactly for odd $h$.
\end{proof}

For a fixed $h$, list the eight occupied interior levels in increasing order.
An eight-bit mask assigns their longitudinal residues. There are $256$ masks.
Changing the origin to the other singleton top event swaps every residue,
i.e.\ replaces the mask by its bitwise complement. We choose the origin so
that the highest occupied interior strand has residue one, giving masks
$128,\ldots,255$. This normalization uses an actual top event and a lattice
translation; it uses neither reflection nor colour complementation.


\begin{corollary}[Standard layer profile]\label{cor:profile}
Suppose the full drift is $(2,-2)$ and, after normalizing the highest odd
level to zero, the odd-class counts at levels $-10,\ldots,0$ are
\[
(2,1,1,1,1,1,1,0,1,1,2),
\]
with no odd classes elsewhere. Every finite-support periodic realization
has period $104$ and the standard read word. The longitudinal residues on
the eight singleton levels need not be specified.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The supplied-word criterion gives growth twelve. Every visited extreme is
odd, by Lemma~\ref{lem:rails}, so the actual width is ten. The supplied
profile is interior-thin. Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
\end{proof}
In particular, specifying all twelve standard class labels gives the
complete-wake uniqueness theorem of version 0.2.0~\cite{KeStandard}.
The direct profile computation used 256 masks, of which 216 failed the
count relaxation; complementary rephasing reduced the remaining forty to
twenty. The present five-profile proof subsumes that classification while
also deriving the missing level. Both proof narratives remain in the source
package; the current full reproduction uses the stronger 76-case inventory.

\section{An all-period upper certificate}\label{sec:upper}
The period is not a supplied search parameter. The following integer certificate
bounds it before chronology is enumerated.

The retained necessary graph has states $(t,h,a)$ for $-10\le t\le0$, with the
required heading axis, where $a$ is a suffix context avoiding $588$ verified
impossible factors of length at most $21$. Every factor contains two consecutive
reads of a physical cell with the same turn and is therefore impossible for
any initial colouring. All placed contexts stay in the stated interval.
The finite recipe gives $54{,}718$ vertices and $90{,}158$ edges. Every periodic
physical trace lifts, after history warmup, to a closed walk in this graph.
The walk need not be a simple cycle; the converse is not assumed.

Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:alpha}
\alpha_t=-2(-1)^t(t+5)^2,
\end{equation}
whose values from $-10$ through zero are
\[
(-50,32,-18,8,-2,0,-2,8,-18,32,-50).
\]
The integer recipe starts all vertex potentials at zero and repeatedly raises
$y_w$ to $\max(y_w,y_v+1+\alpha_t\varepsilon)$ along edges. A guard at $180$
makes this reconstruction a finite procedure; every edge is checked afresh
at termination. The retained certificate passes all inequalities
\begin{equation}\label{eq:edge}
y_v-y_w+\alpha_t\varepsilon\le-1,
\qquad \varepsilon=1\ (\Rr),\quad -1\ (\Ll).
\end{equation}
Its maximum potential is $180$. No floating-point optimizer or solver
infeasibility report is a premise of this certificate.

\begin{lemma}[Profile budgets]\label{lem:budget}
The five profiles of Proposition~\ref{prop:profiles} have respective all-period
bounds $192,168,160,168,192$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Sum~\eqref{eq:edge} along the actual lifted closed walk. Vertex potentials
telescope. Lemma~\ref{lem:criterion} gives the signed turn excess $o_t$ at
level $t$, so
\[
P\le-\sum_t\alpha_t o_t.
\]
Substitution of the five profiles yields the displayed bounds.
\end{proof}

The same graph and potentials certify all profiles. The graph forgets the
longitudinal residue only for this necessary upper bound. The exact search
in the next section retains it. Complete initial black fields are not deleted
or replaced by a chosen template.

\section{Finite residue and count reductions}
At a class adjacent to the processed part, let $I,J$ be the numbers of arrivals
and departures on that side. Number the class events by their stable ranks.
Let $a$ count processed-side arrivals on even ranks and $o\in\{0,1\}$ be the
class's odd indicator. A class length $m$ is count-feasible exactly when
\begin{equation}\label{eq:counts}
 m=\begin{cases}2(I+J-2a)+o,&t\text{ even},\\
 2(J-I+2a)-o,&t\text{ odd},\end{cases}
 \quad m\ge0,\quad 0\le a\le\lceil m/2\rceil,
 \quad0\le I-a\le\lfloor m/2\rfloor.
\end{equation}
Indeed the two rank parities provide precisely these available arrival slots;
the local turn rule fixes departures. This is exact local count feasibility,
not sufficient global chronological realizability.

For a frontier $(b_0,b_1,c_0,c_1)$ of outgoing and incoming edge counts, the new
row sees
\[
(I,J)=\begin{cases}((b_0,b_1),(c_1,c_0)),&t\text{ odd},\\
((b_1,b_0),(c_0,c_1)),&t\text{ even}.
\end{cases}
\]
If its lengths are $m_0,m_1$, the next frontier is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:frontier}
(m_0-J_0,m_1-J_1,m_0-I_0,m_1-I_1).
\end{equation}
Start with $(1,1,1,1)$ at a saturated rail and require the same frontier at
the other rail.

Modulo four, the $a$ term disappears from~\eqref{eq:counts}; hence the residue
frontier is uniquely determined by the wake bits. It can be propagated in
either direction. A wrong terminal residue rejects a mask at every period.
A normalized physical event also satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phase}
i\equiv2r+(t\bmod2)\pmod4.
\end{equation}
This follows directly by alternating vertical and horizontal unit moves.
The four phase groups have $P/4$ events. Their count residues must agree,
and their common residue fixes $P$ modulo sixteen.

\begin{proposition}[Finite input inventory]\label{prop:inventory}
For $h=-9,-7,-5,-3,-1$, respectively, the modular tests leave
$12,20,12,20,12$ normalized masks. Thus $76$ wake assignments remain.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Evaluate~\eqref{eq:counts}--\eqref{eq:frontier} modulo four for the $128$ masks
per profile, and check~\eqref{eq:phase}. The supplement regenerates every
integer calculation in both directions and checks complementary masks.
It also tests all $2304$ masks before normalization and the profile parity
rejection for the four even empty levels. No numerical optimizer is involved.
\end{proof}

For chronology pruning we also use nonnegative completion tables from
\eqref{eq:counts}--\eqref{eq:frontier}. At each frontier retain the least cost
of a counted completion. Because future count transitions depend only on the
frontier, this is an exact minimum for the relaxation and a safe lower bound
for a real completion. Tables are regenerated from the supplied wake; an
incomplete user-provided table is not trusted as an exclusion.

\section{Chronology-exact enumeration and its coverage}
In each full class $(t,r)$, list all events in stable order. Their turns are
fixed: even ranks right, odd ranks left. Enumerate their incoming sides;
literal turning fixes their outgoing sides. In a fixed checker sector an
undirected lattice edge has one direction of use. Therefore its ordered source
and target occurrences match first-to-first, with no free matching permutation.
For class representatives $b_{t,r}=(r,t-r)$ the matching edge has integer voltage
$\delta$ determined by
\[
b_{\rm source}+\text{outgoing unit vector}
=b_{\rm target}+\delta(2,-2).
\]

\begin{lemma}[Exact event-graph test]\label{lem:graph}
A fully joined event graph represents a finite-support periodic trace exactly
when it forms one directed cycle with total voltage one and, along that cycle,
the lifted times $j-Pv$ increase strictly with stable rank in every class.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A real trace yields precisely such ordered edge matches and one phase cycle.
Conversely the voltage identities reconstruct its lattice walk, headings and
drift. The stated time order gives the alternating stabilized word of
Lemma~\ref{lem:criterion}, which constructs a finite seed for the infinite
repetition. A separate full-field parity calculation verifies every accepted seed.
\end{proof}

The searches retain all old sound pruning rules. Count tables only lower-bound
unbuilt cost. A premature directed cycle cannot later join mandatory unbuilt
events. The four phase groups cannot exceed the cap divided by four. Within
a fixed open chain, consecutive reads of the same lifted cell must have
consecutive stable ranks: a missing rank cannot be inserted into a trajectory
segment whose every update is already fixed.

For completeness, the period-dependent time pruning does not assume the final
period equals the cap. On an open chain write
\[
\tau_u=z_{c(u)}+p_u-Pv_u.
\]
Consecutive class ranks require a gap at least four by~\eqref{eq:phase}.
A simple cycle of these difference inequalities on $q$ chain variables has
weight $C+KP$ with integer $K$ and $C\ge5q-n$, where $n$ events have been built.
If $K\ge1$ it is positive at every $P\ge n$; if $K=0$ its sign is constant;
if $K<0$ it decreases with $P$. Thus a positive constraint cycle at the search
cap excludes every admissible smaller period. All operations use exact integers.

No pruning assumes bounded physical memory or a preselected seed box. Only
completed traversals are exclusions. A time or node limit is explicitly
inconclusive.

\subsection{A shorter parity route to the same classification}\label{sec:parity-splice}
The mixed-wake identity in Appendix~\ref{app:mixed} removes three of the five
profiles analytically. In the top-rail frame its $Q$ layers are the odd
levels: translating by $(0,1)$ changes the checker convention and leaves
the lane labels and order unchanged. If $h$ is the missing layer, then
$V=4$ and $J=(h+37)/2$. The condition $J+V/2\equiv1\pmod2$ leaves precisely
$h=-7,-3$. It is independent of all eight interior residue choices.
For $h=-7$, the twenty retained normalized chronological exclusions at cap
168 apply. The $h=-3$ case is the standard layer-profile theorem, including
its complete residue classification. This yields an alternative proof using
those two retained subcertificates. The full five-profile computation above
remains a complete route with the same conclusion.

More generally, put the full extreme rails at $-2k,0$ and one odd class at
each interior level except $h=-2k+1+2j$. Heading restoration forces $k$ odd.
Then $V=k-1$ and $J=(k+2)(k-1)/2+j$, so the identity requires $j$ odd,
equivalently $h\equiv1\pmod4$. This derivation supplies the necessary
single-gap parity test at every such width.

\section{Evidence, reproducibility and remaining questions}\label{sec:verification}
The core theorem is computer-assisted: its logical coverage is a written
mathematical argument and its finite claims are checked by integer programs.
The supplemental source hashes identify the executed programs and the
complete run records identify the finite exclusions.

The supplement contains the unchanged inherited count routines, the two
ordered-event traversal directions, the local rank check, the upper-bound
recipe, all parameterized sources actually executed, and the complete native
records. A regenerated count implementation is cross-checked with the retained
one. The small shard controls compare their union with an unsplit positive
search. The full replay checks every case, all eight final shards, and the
unique finite seed. Single active-cell deletions and a far-ahead blocker are
rejected by the independent infinite verifier.

\begin{lstlisting}
python3 wake_synthesis/reproduce.py --jobs 4
python3 wake_synthesis/reproduce.py --quick
\end{lstlisting}
The second command explicitly skips the 76-case exclusion replay, while checking
its upper certificate, count tables and controls. The default
is full replay, including the added periodic-growth and mixed-wake checks.
The option \texttt{--extensions-only} replays the arithmetic modules, including
the general quadratic character, analytic unit-diagonal regressions and the
thin-profile parity reduction, while retaining the classification evidence.
An additional retained 256-lift profile classification has its own full
entrypoint in \texttt{PROFILE\_REPLAY.md}; it is not needed by the stronger
thin-wake theorem. Python's standard library and a C++17 compiler are sufficient;
no linear optimizer, SAT solver or network access is required. Resource-limited
exploratory runs are retained as such and are not used as negative evidence.

The theorem isolates a genuinely narrower residual periodic problem: a
nonstandard highway retaining drift $(2,-2)$, growth twelve and width ten must
have a thicker interior wake. It remains necessary to determine whether such
wakes exist, whether different numerical invariants are possible, and whether
all finite seeds eventually become periodic. Those questions are not answered
by the present classification.

\appendix
\section{Wake-dependent period bounds and inverse synthesis}\label{app:general}
The standard-wake classification is one instance of a general inverse
problem. Prescribe a non-axis drift $d$, an initial heading, and a finite
set $O\subset\Z^2/\Z d$ of odd classes. We seek a periodic word and finite
seed with exactly these data. The following bounds, developed in the first
version~\cite{previous}, determine a finite search range directly from the
input wake.

Lemma~\ref{lem:criterion} gives $\#\R-\#\Lturn=|O|=:g$.
Heading restoration implies $g\equiv0\pmod4$, $P$ even and $d_x+d_y$ even.
For $d=(m,-m)$, the complete class label is $(x+y,x\bmod m)$.
The checker-sector invariant $\sigma$ is determined by the supplied pose.

\subsection{An integer clock for diagonal budgets}
After rotating a diagonal drift parallel to $(1,-1)$, the transverse
coordinate $x+y$ and heading return after each period. A clock using these
quantities has a cancelling
head correction: its period gain comes from the wake. We recall the unit
increment identity of~\cite{clock} before computing that gain.

Fix an integer $c$, set $s=x+y-c$, and put
\[
 f(s)=3^{|s|}-1,\qquad
 \epsilon(h)=\begin{cases}+1,&h=N,E,\\-1,&h=S,W,\end{cases}\qquad
 G(s,e)=\begin{cases}\tfrac12 f(s),&es\ge0,\\-\tfrac32 f(s),&es<0.\end{cases}
\]
Use the integer clock of~\cite{clock} on the complete finite state
\begin{equation}\label{eq:integer}
 \Theta_c(X)=-\sigma(X)\sum_{q\in B}\chi(q)f(q_x+q_y-c)
             +G(p_x+p_y-c,\epsilon(h)).
\end{equation}
Since $f(s)$ is even, $\Theta_c$ is an exactly computable integer.

\begin{lemma}[Integer clock, Ke~\cite{clock}]\label{thm:clock}
For every finite-support state and integer centre $c$, with $e,e'$ the before
and after values of $\epsilon$ and $s=p_x+p_y-c$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gain}
 \Theta_c(FX)-\Theta_c(X)
   =1+4f(s)\ind_{\{e'\ne e,\ es<0\}}\ \ge1.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $\delta=+1$ on $\R$ and $-1$ on $\Lturn$. Directly checking the two colours
at four headings gives $\delta\sigma\chi(p)=ee'$, and the outgoing step gives
$s'=s+e'$. Thus the black-field increment is $-ee'f(s)$. Let $G_\pm(s)=G(s,\pm1)$.
The elementary recurrences are
\begin{align*}
 G_+(s+1)-G_+(s)&=f(s)+1,&G_-(s-1)-G_-(s)&=f(s)+1,\\
 G_-(s)-G_+(s)&=-2\sgn(s)f(s).
\end{align*}
If $e'=e$ the total gain is one. For $+1\to-1$ it is
$1+2f(s)+G_-(s)-G_+(s)$, equal to one for $s\ge0$ and to $1+4f(s)$ for $s<0$.
The other reversal gives the reflected cases. This includes $s=0$.
\end{proof}
\subsection{Diagonal wake budgets and crossing refinement}
Rotate a diagonal drift to $d=(m,-m)$, $m>0$, and use the transformed heading.
Let $t=x+y$, let $o_t$ count odd classes at level $t$, and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:A}
 A_t=-\sigma(-1)^t o_t,\qquad
 K_c(O)=\sum_t A_t(3^{|t-c|}-1).
\end{equation}
\begin{theorem}[Wake-determined diagonal period]\label{thm:diagonal}
Every realization of this wake obeys $P\le K_c(O)$ for every integer $c$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Over one period the head term of \eqref{eq:integer} cancels, since both $t$ and
the heading return to their initial values. The signed cell toggles give
$-\sigma\sum_i\chi(q_i)f(t_i-c)\delta_i$.
The weight is constant in each full $d$-class. Lemma~\ref{lem:criterion} makes
its net $\delta$ count one on odd classes and zero on even classes. The exact
period increment is therefore $K_c(O)$. Summing \eqref{eq:gain} proves the bound.
The unchanged part of the full field cancels algebraically; it is not deleted.
\end{proof}

Here is an independent geometric derivation, which also supplies a refinement.
The finite projected period is a closed walk with increments $\pm1$ in $t$.
Let $f_t$ count upward crossings of the cut $(t,t+1)$; it also counts downward
crossings. Let $m_t$ count visits to $t$, and $u_t$ count passages entering and
leaving in the increasing direction. Flow conservation equates this with the
number of decreasing-through passages. The fixed arrival axis and the two
turn choices give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:flow}
 m_t=f_{t-1}+f_t=A_t+4u_t,\qquad
 0\le u_t\le\min(f_{t-1},f_t).
\end{equation}
For example, at vertical-arrival levels, right turns are through passages and
left turns reverse the projection, giving $\#\R-\#\Lturn=4u_t-m_t$; horizontal
arrival reverses that sign. Substituting $u_t\le f_{t-1}$ into
$f_t=A_t+4u_t-f_{t-1}$ gives the first inequality below; $u_t\le f_t$
gives the second:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:recurrence}
 f_t\le3f_{t-1}+A_t,\qquad f_{t-1}\le3f_t+A_t.
\end{equation}
Let $\ell,h$ be the extreme wake levels. Outside them $A_t=0$; starting past the
finite support of the projected walk and applying \eqref{eq:recurrence} inward
forces $f_{\ell-1}=f_h=0$. Thus the periodic trace lies between the extreme wake levels. An empty wake forces every
$f_t=0$, impossible for a nonempty period.

Starting from these two zero boundary values, set
\[
 L_{\ell-1}=0,\quad L_t=3L_{t-1}+A_t,\qquad
 R_h=0,\quad R_t=3R_{t+1}+A_{t+1},\quad C_t=\min(L_t,R_t).
\]
Then $0\le f_t\le C_t$ for $\ell\le t<h$. If a $C_t$ is negative, the wake is
impossible; otherwise
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cuts}
 P=2\sum_t f_t\le2\sum_t C_t.
\end{equation}
Moreover $d_x=\sigma\sum_t(-1)^t f_t$: on a cut with $\sigma(-1)^t=1$, the
upward horizontal moves contribute $+f_t$ to $x$; on the other cuts the downward
horizontal moves contribute $-f_t$. If $C_+$ and $C_-$ sum the corresponding
positive and negative cut bounds, then also
\begin{equation}\label{eq:direction}
 P\le4C_+-2d_x,\qquad P\le4C_-+2d_x.
\end{equation}

\begin{corollary}[Width and growth bound]
Writing $W=h-\ell$, a diagonal realization has even $W$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coarse}
 P\le g(3^{W/2}-1).
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
At a transverse extreme every passage reverses the projection. The fixed arrival
axis makes all letters in an extremal class equal. Lemma~\ref{lem:criterion}
therefore forces exactly one $\R$ per such class. Both extremes have the same
arrival axis, so $W$ is even; this is the usual extremal rigidity argument.
Take $c=(\ell+h)/2$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:diagonal}, discard negative coefficients
and use $\sum_t\max(A_t,0)\le g$ to obtain \eqref{eq:coarse}.
\end{proof}

For the normalized standard wake, $\sigma=-1$ and the coefficients at
$t=-10,\ldots,0$ are
\[
 A=(2,-1,1,-1,1,-1,1,0,1,-1,2).
\]
The cut bounds are
$C=(2,5,16,47,142,145,48,16,5,2)$, with sum $428$.
Thus \eqref{eq:cuts} gives $P\le856$, as does \eqref{eq:A} with centre $c=-5$.
The chronological constraints in Section~\ref{sec:upper} strengthen this
budget to the cutoff used in the complete classification.

\subsection{General non-axis drift by reciprocal moments}
For a general drift, use a transverse linear coordinate constant along it.
An exponential weight and its reciprocal give complementary period budgets;
Cauchy--Schwarz then bounds the number of steps independently of their
individual weights.

Rotate the drift to $(a_0,-b_0)$, $a_0,b_0>0$. Put
$k=\gcd(a_0,b_0)$, $\eta=(b_0/k,a_0/k)$, and choose rational $r>1$ such that,
with $a=r^{\eta_x}$ and $b=r^{\eta_y}$,
\[
 \kappa=1-\frac2{a+1}-\frac2{b+1}>0.
\]
The choice $r=4$ always works. Define $w(q)=r^{\eta\cdot q}$, and
\[
 (\gamma_N,\gamma_E,\gamma_S,\gamma_W)
 =\left(\frac2{b+1},\frac2{a+1},-\frac{2b}{b+1},-\frac{2a}{a+1}\right).
\]
The one-sided potentials are
\[
 \Psi_+(X)=-\sigma\sum_{q\in B}\chi(q)w(q)+\gamma_h w(p),\qquad
 \Psi_-(X)=-\sigma\sum_{q\in B}\frac{\chi(q)}{w(q)}+\frac{\gamma_{h+2}}{w(p)}.
\]
Substitution of the current-cell flip gives $\Delta\Psi_+=\kappa w(p)$ in six
heading/colour cases and $(\kappa+4)w(p)$ in $S/\Lturn$ and $W/\R$.
The half-turn formula gives $\Delta\Psi_-\ge\kappa/w(p)$. These local identities
are the one-sided exponential clock construction, included to make the new
period argument self-contained.

Define the two finite wake moments, using any representatives of $O$,
\[
 A_+=-\sigma\sum_{q\in O}\chi(q)r^{\eta\cdot q},\qquad
 A_-=-\sigma\sum_{q\in O}\chi(q)r^{-\eta\cdot q}.
\]
They are independent of the representatives since $\eta\cdot d=0$ and
$d_x+d_y$ is even. The head terms cancel over a period, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:criterion} gives exact increments $A_+$ and $A_-$. Thus both are
positive and $\kappa\sum_i w(q_i)\le A_+$,
$\kappa\sum_i w(q_i)^{-1}\le A_-$. Cauchy--Schwarz yields:
\begin{theorem}[Reciprocal wake bound]\label{thm:moment}
Every realization with the specified non-axis drift and wake satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:moment}
 P^2\le \frac{A_+A_-}{\kappa^2}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
All quantities are rational. The integer square root of the floor of the right
side is a computable cutoff. Changing the transverse centre scales $A_+$ and
$A_-$ reciprocally, leaving the product unchanged. For axis drift the normal has
a zero coordinate, so this construction cannot give positive $\kappa$.

\subsection{All-period synthesis and detour obstruction}
\begin{theorem}[Period-unknown inverse synthesis]\label{thm:inverse}
Given a non-axis integer drift, an initial heading and a finite odd-class wake,
existence of a finite-support periodic realization is decidable. A positive
answer can produce its word and an explicit finite seed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
First reject odd $d_x+d_y$, growth $|O|$ not divisible by four, or a
nonpositive moment $A_+$ or $A_-$. Otherwise compute
Theorem~\ref{thm:moment}'s cutoff, additionally using
Theorem~\ref{thm:diagonal} and \eqref{eq:cuts}--\eqref{eq:direction} in the
diagonal case. A negative crossing bound also rejects the input.
Enumerate every nonempty word up to the least derived bound.
Check its heading and drift, apply Lemma~\ref{lem:criterion}, and require the
exact odd-class set. A passing word constructs a finite seed; complete finite
exhaustion gives a negative answer. Completeness follows from the period bound.
\end{proof}
The all-future verifier of Section~\ref{sec:verification} independently
checks the constructed finite seed. For cutoff $B$, enumeration visits at
most $2^{B+1}-2$ nonempty binary words; the period bound guarantees termination.

\begin{corollary}[No wake-preserving closed-pose insertion]
Suppose realizable words $u,v$ have the same non-axis drift, and $v$ is obtained
by inserting nonempty detours into $u$, each returning to the same position and
heading while leaving the original steps unchanged. Then
\[
 A_+(O_v)>A_+(O_u),\qquad A_-(O_v)>A_-(O_u).
\]
Thus their signed transverse wake cannot be identical. For diagonal drift,
$K_c(O_v)-K_c(O_u)\ge |v|-|u|>0$ for every integer $c$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Every retained original step has the same position, heading and letter; hence
it has the same local clock increment. Every inserted step has strictly positive
increment in both one-sided clocks, and at least one in the integer clock.
Sum over the words and use the exact wake-increment equalities.
\end{proof}
Thus any such realizable detour requires an additional wake budget.
