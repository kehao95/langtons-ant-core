\begin{abstract}
The permanent wake of a periodic Langton ant can determine its motion
uniquely. We prove that with drift $(2,-2)$ and the twelve complete odd
translation classes of the classical highway,
every finite-support periodic realization is the standard $104$-step highway,
up to phase and pose. The period is determined by the wake rather than
supplied as a hypothesis. The proof combines a finite graph of necessary
short-factor constraints with an exact ordered-event representation.
An integer potential on the first graph bounds every candidate period by
$168$; exhaustive chronological matching on the second leaves one normalized
word. Consecutive-visit ranks and separation of fixed trajectory chains reduce
the verified enumeration from $40{,}227{,}976$ to $8{,}580{,}751$ prefixes. More generally, we retain
wake-dependent period bounds for every non-axis drift, giving a terminating
inverse-synthesis algorithm and an obstruction to wake-preserving detours.
All finite certificates can be regenerated, and the accepted word is checked
on an explicit finite seed for its entire future.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords.} Langton's ant; periodic highway; finite support;
symbolic dynamics; computer-assisted proof; inverse synthesis.

\section{Introduction}
A periodic Langton ant leaves a geometric record of its motion: the permanent
black strands in its wake. The classical highway repeats after $104$ updates,
translating by $(2,-2)$ in a suitable orientation. Its wake records which
translation classes have been visited an odd number of times, while the
motion also specifies the order of those visits. This paper proves that the
complete standard wake determines that order uniquely among finite-support
periodic realizations with the same drift.

The inverse problem starts with the wake and seeks the periodic motion that
produces it. Its first difficulty is that the period is unknown. Its second
is chronological: each visit flips a cell, so visits to the same physical
cell must alternate right and left turns. Counts of visits give useful
necessary conditions, but the order links different phases and different
periods. Recovering the highway requires both a period budget and exact
control of these links.

\paragraph{Main result.}
For the twelve standard classes $\Ostd$ specified in
Section~\ref{sec:model}, every heading-restoring periodic realization from a
finite black set, with drift $(2,-2)$ and wake $\Ostd$, is the standard
$104$-step highway. A periodic suffix satisfying these hypotheses therefore
has the standard future trace, with any stationary unread cells retained.
The theorem classifies motion under its complete wake; its hypotheses are
stated directly in terms of the drift and translation classes.

Two complementary reductions make the classification finite.
Short factors that contradict the literal colour-update rule define a finite
context graph. Every candidate trajectory projects to a closed walk in this
graph. An integer potential bounds its period by $168$.
We then enumerate ordered visits within the complete translation classes,
retaining longitudinal residues and the chronological order across periods.
This second representation is exact, and its completed enumeration leaves a
single word. Consecutive-rank and chain-separation lemmas strengthen the search while
preserving its exact coverage.

The uniqueness theorem completes the standard-wake problem posed in the
first version of this preprint, \emph{Reading the Wake}~\cite{previous}.
The general period bounds and inverse-synthesis theorem from that version
remain in Appendix~\ref{app:general}. For every prescribed finite wake,
non-axis drift and initial heading, a computable cutoff makes the inverse
problem decidable and constructs a finite seed whenever one exists.
The present theorem carries that programme through for the standard wake.

\paragraph{Related work.}
Gajardo studies Langton's ant through symbolic dynamics and forbidden
factors~\cite{Gajardo2003}. Jillhewar gives an exact realization criterion
for a supplied periodic word, a constructive finite seed, and structural
restrictions on finite-support highways~\cite{Jillhewar2026}. His
width-four analysis also gives a special period--drift bound~\cite{widthfour}.
We recall the realization criterion with its proof, then develop the
integer upper certificate and complete chronological classification for
$\Ostd$. The general wake budgets use the integer clock from the companion
escape paper~\cite{clock}. These dependencies separate the supplied-word
test, the quantitative period bounds, and the standard-wake uniqueness
proved here.

exact event representation, and completed classification.
Section~\ref{sec:verification} describes the independent all-future checks.
The appendices retain the general synthesis theory, the explicit witness,

\section{Model, normalization, and the standard wake}\label{sec:model}
A complete state is $X=(B,p,h)$, where $B\subset\Z^2$ is finite,
$p\in\Z^2$, and $h\in\{\mathrm N,\mathrm E,\mathrm S,\mathrm W\}$.
All cells outside $B$ are white. With headings encoded by $0,1,2,3$ in that
order, the update $F$ is
\[
 h'=h+1-2\ind_B(p)\pmod4,\qquad
 B'=B\diff\{p\},\qquad p'=p+e_{h'}.
\]
Time $i$ denotes the state before update $i$. Write $\R$ for reading white
and turning right, and $\Lturn$ for reading black and turning left.
Every assertion below uses this convention.

A word $w=w_0\cdots w_{P-1}$ gives a prescribed walk, with pre-update positions
$q_i$ and headings $h_i$. A \emph{periodic realization} is a complete finite
state whose entire future turn stream is $w^\omega$. We require $h_P=h_0$
and nonzero drift $d=q_P-q_0$. The full black field includes the growing wake and any stationary unread cells.

Put $\chi(x,y)=(-1)^{x+y}$. The quantity
\[
 \sigma(X)=
 \begin{cases}
 \chi(p),&h\text{ is vertical},\\
 -\chi(p),&h\text{ is horizontal}
 \end{cases}
\]
is invariant, since location parity and heading axis both change on each
update. We fix $d=(2,-2)$. Lemma~\ref{lem:extreme} will derive $\sigma=-1$
and a canonical start at the origin facing east from the prescribed wake.

\begin{definition}[Complete odd-class wake]
Two positions belong to the same drift class if their difference lies in
$\Z d$. For $d=(2,-2)$ the complete class label is
\[
 (t,r)=(x+y,\ x\bmod2).
\]
The wake of a word is the set of classes containing an odd number of its
phase positions. The prescribed standard wake is
\begin{align}
 \Ostd=\{&
 (-10,0),(-10,1),(-9,1),(-8,1),(-7,0),(-6,0), \notag\\
 &(-5,0),(-4,1),(-2,0),(-1,1),(0,0),(0,1)\}. \label{eq:wake}
\end{align}
\end{definition}
The quotient uses the full drift. Repeating a word twice, for example,
doubles its drift and changes the quotient in which its wake is specified.

\begin{theorem}[Uniqueness under the standard wake]\label{thm:main}
Every finite-support-realizable, heading-restoring periodic word with drift
$(2,-2)$ and wake \eqref{eq:wake} is the standard $P104$ word.
In the normalized phase at the top class $(0,0)$ its period is $104$ and
its word is exactly $w_*$ displayed in Appendix~\ref{app:witness}.
The corresponding translations, quarter turns, and phase changes give the
equivalent geometric statement.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:entrance}
If a finite initial state has an eventual periodic suffix with the above
drift and complete wake, up to the stated normalization, then its future is
eventually the standard highway.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The black field at any finite suffix boundary is still a complete finite set.
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:main} at that boundary.
\end{proof}

The conclusion identifies the read trace. Complete initial fields may differ
on cells that the future trace never reads. The twelve class labels in
\eqref{eq:wake} specify both transverse levels and longitudinal residues.

\section{Finite-support realization and extreme classes}\label{sec:criterion}
We recall the chronological criterion of~\cite[Theorem 3.1 and Remark 3.2]{Jillhewar2026}.
The proof identifies exactly where finite support is used.

Fix a class and write its phase positions as $q_i=b+a_i d$.
The visit to the physical cell $b+n d$ at phase $i$ occurs at time
\[
 (n-a_i)P+i=nP+(i-Pa_i),\qquad n\ge a_i.
\]
Thus stable visit order is increasing $i-Pa_i$, or equivalently decreasing
$a_i$, with increasing $i$ to break ties.

\begin{lemma}[Supplied-word criterion]\label{lem:criterion}
A heading-restoring word of nonzero drift has a finite-support periodic
realization if and only if the stable word in every class is
$\R,\Lturn,\R,\Lturn,\ldots$, starting with $\R$.
A finite seed can be constructed from the word.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For all sufficiently large $n$, every phase in the class occurs at $b+n d$.
All but finitely many such cells are initially white. Their first turn is
therefore $\R$, and each successive reading flips the colour.

Conversely, at $b+n d$ the available phases are obtained from the stable
order by deleting all groups with $a_i>n$. This deletes a prefix and leaves
an alternating suffix. For $\min_i a_i\le n<\max_i a_i$, place a black cell
precisely when the number of deleted phases is odd. Put no other black cells
in this class. The suffix then starts with the required colour.
There are only finitely many boundary cells and classes. Induction over
literal time proves execution of every prescribed update.
\end{proof}

Consequently each class has signed excess $\#\R-\#\Lturn$ equal to its
indicator in the odd wake. This supplies the exact balances used below.

\begin{lemma}[Extremal singleton classes]\label{lem:extreme}
For a realization with wake \eqref{eq:wake}, $\sigma=-1$ and every phase
position satisfies $-10\le t\le0$. Every class visited on either extreme level has exactly one
event, a right turn. Top events arrive heading east; bottom events arrive west.
There is a unique event of the top class $(0,0)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
At the highest visited level there is no predecessor or successor at a
higher level. Arrival and departure directions are therefore forced by
the axis invariant. Every event of an extreme class has the same turn.
Lemma~\ref{lem:criterion} permits only a singleton right turn in that class,
so the extreme class must be odd. The same argument applies at the lowest
level. Since the extreme levels of the given odd wake are $-10$ and $0$,
they are also the trace extremes. At the top level $t=0$, a right turn
that both arrives from and returns to the lower level must enter eastward
and leave southward. Hence $\sigma=-1$. The bottom right turn enters
westward and leaves northward. Both residue classes at each extreme
belong to \eqref{eq:wake}, and hence occur exactly once.
\end{proof}
A top event in class $(0,0)$ has position $(2k,-2k)$. Changing the phase to
that event and translating by $-k d$ preserves every class label in
\eqref{eq:wake}. This gives the canonical phase in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. Choosing the other
top event as origin gives the equivalent description with both residues swapped.

\section{A finite certificate for an all-period bound}\label{sec:upper}
The upper bound uses a finite graph that retains transverse position,
heading and a short turn context. Every actual periodic trace lifts to a
closed walk in this graph. Its integer edge inequalities will then bound
the length of every such trace.

\subsection{Forbidden factors and geometric context}
If a proposed finite factor reads one physical cell twice consecutively
with the same turn, the factor cannot occur on any initial colouring.
Generate physically consistent first-return words of at most $20$ updates,
append the initial turn again, and include complemented words.
The resulting $588$ forbidden factors have length at most $21$.
Each is separately checked by geometric reconstruction; completeness of this
list is unnecessary.

The contexts are the empty word and the proper viable prefix contexts of
these factors. After a new letter, retain the longest suffix that is again a
context, rejecting a forbidden suffix. There are $4{,}931$ contexts.
A vertex is $(t,h,a)$: level, heading, and context. Retain only headings
compatible with $\sigma=-1$ and contexts whose entire geometric placement
lies in $-10\le t\le0$. The latter placement is recovered by reversing the
net displacement of the context and rotating to its current heading.
A graph edge is one literal turn and unit move with the corresponding
context update.

The regenerated graph has $54{,}718$ vertices and $90{,}158$ edges.
After context warmup, a periodic trajectory lifts to a length-$P$ closed
walk: its transverse position, heading and suffix context repeat after
one period. Vertices and edges may be revisited.

\begin{lemma}[Degree peeling preserves every closed walk]\label{lem:peeling}
Repeatedly delete a vertex with zero incoming or zero outgoing degree in the
currently retained graph, together with its incident edges. Every closed
walk in the original graph remains in the final graph.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If a vertex of a closed walk were deleted, consider the first deleted vertex
on that walk. Its predecessor and successor would still be present, so it
would still have an incoming and an outgoing edge, a contradiction.
\end{proof}
This reduction retains $24{,}424$ vertices and $37{,}330$ edges.
A separate reverse-insertion check verifies the deletion order. We verify
the upper potential on this smaller graph; Lemma~\ref{lem:peeling} preserves
the coverage needed for the period bound.

\subsection{An exact integer potential}
For levels $t=-10,\ldots,0$ define
\[
 \alpha_t=-2(-1)^t(t+5)^2,
\]
that is,
\[
 \alpha=(-50,32,-18,8,-2,0,-2,8,-18,32,-50).
\]
For an edge $v\to w$ with source level $t$, put
$\varepsilon=1$ for $\R$ and $-1$ for $\Lturn$.

\begin{lemma}[Finite upper certificate]\label{lem:uppercert}
The regenerated graph admits integers $0\le y_v\le180$ such that on every edge
\begin{equation}
 y_v-y_w+\alpha_t\varepsilon\le-1. \label{eq:dual}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}[Computer-assisted proof]
Initialize all $y_v$ to zero and repeatedly enforce
$y_w\ge y_v+1+\alpha_t\varepsilon$ along queued outgoing edges.
Abort, rather than accept, if any value exceeds $180$.
With this cap the construction terminates: every change raises an integer,
and there are at most $180|V|$ changes. Once the queue is empty, a separate
scan verifies \eqref{eq:dual} on every edge, without relying on the
construction algorithm. The supplement regenerates the reduced graph and its potential, records
the construction statistics, and checks each retained inequality using
integer arithmetic. The reconstructed potential ranges from $0$ to $129$,
and the maximum left-hand side is $-1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[All-period upper bound]\label{prop:upper}
Every realization in Theorem~\ref{thm:main} has $P\le168$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The per-level odd counts are
\[
 o=(2,1,1,1,1,1,1,0,1,1,2).
\]
Summing \eqref{eq:dual} on the lifted closed walk cancels the vertex terms.
Lemma~\ref{lem:criterion} identifies the signed turn excess on layer $t$ with
$o_t$. Direct integer arithmetic gives $\sum_t\alpha_t o_t=-168$.
Thus $-168\le-P$.
\end{proof}
The upper graph depends only on the per-level counts. The next stage uses
the full class labels in \eqref{eq:wake} to determine which bounded walks
are realizable.

\section{An exact ordered-event representation}\label{sec:events}
In each complete class $(t,r)$ let $m$ be its number of events and index
them by stable rank $k=0,\ldots,m-1$. The turn is right at even rank and
left at odd rank. Let $a_k=1$ indicate arrival from $t-1$, and $d_k=1$
indicate departure to $t-1$. The literal rule is
\[
 d_k=
 \begin{cases}
 a_k\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}}(k\bmod2),&t\text{ even},\\
 1-(a_k\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}}(k\bmod2)),&t\text{ odd}.
 \end{cases}
\]
At even levels arrival is east or west according as $a_k=1$ or $0$;
at odd levels it is north or south.

Every directed lattice edge has one direction of use in the fixed checker
sector. Its source occurrences and target occurrences match in stable
order, first to first and so on: physical time increases by one on that edge,
and passage to the target representative changes lifted time by a constant.
There is no free matching permutation.

Use representatives $b_{t,r}=(r,t-r)$.
An edge has integer voltage $\delta$ determined by
\[
 b_{\rm source}+e_{\rm outgoing}=b_{\rm target}+\delta d.
\]
A vertical edge has voltage zero. East moves from residue $1$ to $0$ have
voltage $1$; west moves from $0$ to $1$ have voltage $-1$.
Other horizontal edges have voltage zero.

\begin{proposition}[Exact event-graph test]\label{prop:eventtest}
A complete matched graph represents a normalized realization if and only if
it forms one directed cycle of total voltage $1$, and the lifted times
$j_i-Pv_i$, read along that cycle from the top event $(0,0)$, strictly
increase with rank in every class. Here $j_i$ is cycle index and $v_i$
is accumulated voltage.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A real trajectory gives these matches and times, so necessity follows.
Conversely, following the proposed cycle reconstructs a literal walk with
the correct headings and drift $d$. The lifted-time test makes its stable
class order exactly the rank order. The turn assigned to every rank
therefore satisfies Lemma~\ref{lem:criterion}, which constructs the finite
seed. The explicit seed is then checked independently as described in
Section~\ref{sec:verification}.
\end{proof}
In the normalized gauge each word determines its event graph. Enumerating
all such graphs therefore enumerates words, not merely a selection of seeds.

\section{Exhaustion without enumerating all binary words}\label{sec:search}
The search builds complete classes one transverse layer at a time.
It enumerates ordered arrival strings, joins forced edge occurrences
immediately, and rejects partial graphs by necessary conditions.
A completed traversal supplies the finite classification.

\subsection{Exact count completion and safe merging of count histories}
Suppose a new class has $I$ arrivals and $J$ departures on the already
processed side. Let $o=\ind_{\Ostd}(t,r)$ and let $a$ count processed-side
arrivals at even ranks. Complement both side bits when the construction
runs downward. The possible class lengths are exactly
\begin{equation}
 m=
 \begin{cases}
 2(I+J-2a)+o,&t\text{ even},\\
 2(J-I+2a)-o,&t\text{ odd},
 \end{cases}\qquad
 0\le a\le\lceil m/2\rceil,\quad
 0\le I-a\le\lfloor m/2\rfloor. \label{eq:counts}
\end{equation}
These formulas follow by counting the outgoing bits in the local rule.
Conversely, the inequalities choose the required number of ones at even
and odd ranks, so they are an exact count feasibility test.

A boundary stores its two outgoing and two incoming counts. Transitions
between boundaries retain the residue swap on horizontal edges.
A dynamic program merges count histories with the same boundary, keeping
the smallest completion cost. Every future count transition depends only
on that boundary, so the minimum gives a lower bound on the remaining
number of events. The event search separately retains all forced matches
and chronological constraints.

Both directions of this count program show a minimum total cost $104$.
At cutoff $104$ the top-down boundary-state counts are
\[
 1,1,6,81,174,92,65,19,8,1.
\]
The required boundary just above the bottom has minimum cost $102$;
the two bottom singletons add $2$. Any putative period below $104$ has
all partial nonnegative costs below $104$ and is therefore covered by this
finite calculation. Twelve minimum count profiles remain for the exact chronological search.

\subsection{Phase congruence}
Induction in \eqref{eq:counts} fixes the class-length residues modulo four.
From bottom to top they are
\[
 (1,1),(0,3),(2,3),(1,2),(1,0),(3,0),
 (2,3),(2,2),(3,2),(0,3),(1,1).
\]
For the normalized physical walk,
\[
 i\bmod4=2(x_i\bmod2)+(x_i+y_i\bmod2).
\]
This follows directly by checking the four update phases starting east
at the origin. A heading-restoring period with horizontal displacement $2$
has an even number $P/2$ of horizontal moves, so $4\mid P$.
Each of the four phase groups contains $P/4$ events.
Their displayed residue sums are $(10,6,10,10)$, all $2\bmod4$. Hence
\begin{equation}
 P\equiv8\pmod{16}. \label{eq:congruence}
\end{equation}
Together with the upper bound and the count minimum, the only candidate
lengths are $104,120,136,152,168$.

\subsection{Chronological difference constraints}
A partial graph that closes a directed component before the final layer
cannot be completed into one cycle: required future events could no longer
join that fixed component. All other components are directed open chains.

On one chain write
\[
 \tau_u=z_{\mathrm{chain}(u)}+p_u-Pv_u,
\]
where $p_u$ is its position in the chain and $v_u$ its accumulated voltage.
Consecutive ranks in a class have the same phase modulo four.
Their strict stable order therefore requires a gap at least four:
\begin{equation}
 z_{\mathrm{chain}(v)}
 \ge z_{\mathrm{chain}(u)}
       +4+p_u-p_v+(v_v-v_u)P. \label{eq:difference}
\end{equation}
A positive cycle of these difference constraints is impossible.

The final period is not yet known. Let $n$ be the number of constructed
events and $L$ the search cap. It is safe to test \eqref{eq:difference}
at $P=L$, as the following argument shows.
For a simple cycle on $q$ chain variables, its total weight is $C+KP$,
where $K\in\Z$. Each distinct chain contributes one difference of two
indices. Its index range is its length minus one, and the $q$ chains
together contain at most $n$ vertices. Thus
\[
 5q-n\le C\le n+3q.
\]
If $K\ge1$, the cycle is positive for every $P\ge n$.
If $K=0$, its sign is independent of $P$.
If $K\le-1$, its weight decreases with $P$.
A positive cycle at $L$ consequently rules out every $n\le P\le L$.
This proves that evaluating the constraints at the search cap is a sound
pruning rule. All checks use exact signed integers.

\subsection{A local refinement: consecutive ranks on a fixed chain}\label{sec:rank}
A fixed chain of matched edges is already a contiguous piece of physical
motion. Its repeated visits to a cell must therefore occupy consecutive
positions in that cell's ordered visit list.

\begin{lemma}[Consecutive-visit rank pruning]\label{lem:rank}
Let an open chain of fixed edges be part of a realizable event graph.
Lift it to the lattice by accumulating edge voltages.
If two consecutive occurrences of one physical lattice cell in this chain
have stable class ranks $k$ and $\ell$, then $\ell=k+1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Translate a sufficiently late occurrence of the chain along the periodic
trajectory. Every class involved is then in the stable finite-support
regime. The chain is a contiguous sequence of actual updates; its length
does not exceed the final period. Between the stated occurrences there is
no intervening visit to that physical cell. They are therefore consecutive
in the cell's stable visit list. Its list is indexed by consecutive ranks
$0,\ldots,m-1$, proving the assertion.
\end{proof}
In particular, equal turn bits are forbidden, but mere bit alternation is
weaker: a proposed jump from rank $0$ to rank $3$ would alternate turns
while omitting required visits.

For implementation, a node of label $(t,r)$ at accumulated voltage $v$
has lifted coordinate $(r+2v,t-r-2v)$. A stamped array records the previous
rank at each cell while traversing one open chain. Separate chains receive
separate stamps; they are not assumed to share an absolute placement.
This check is inserted after premature closed cycles are rejected and
before the more expensive difference-constraint solver.


\subsection{Separation of fixed trajectory chains}\label{sec:separation}
Open chains also constrain each other's possible placement in time.
Each is a contiguous block of updates, and distinct chains contain disjoint
phase events. Their copies in a completed periodic trajectory therefore
occupy disjoint time intervals.

\begin{lemma}[Chain separation]\label{lem:separation}
Suppose consecutive ranks of a class lie at events $u$ and $v$ in distinct
open chains $A$ and $B$. Let $\ell_A$ be the number of events in $A$.
Their chronological constraint strengthens to
\begin{equation}
 z_B\ge z_A+
 \max\{4+p_u-p_v,\ell_A\}+(v_v-v_u)P. \label{eq:separation}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Shift sufficiently late copies of the two chains by whole periods so that
$u$ and $v$ refer to the same physical cell. Their respective root times
are $z_A-Pv_u$ and $z_B-Pv_v$, up to a common epoch offset.
Stable rank order places $u$ before $v$. Since the two contiguous chain
intervals are disjoint, the whole interval for $A$ precedes that for $B$.
Thus $z_B-Pv_v\ge z_A-Pv_u+\ell_A$.
Combining this inequality with \eqref{eq:difference} proves the claim.
\end{proof}
The stronger constraint is especially convenient at an unknown period.
Each interchain edge has weight $C_e+K_eP$ with
$C_e\ge\ell_A\ge1$. A cycle has weight $C+KP$ with $C>0$.
If $K\ge0$, it is positive for every positive $P$; if $K<0$, it increases
as $P$ decreases. A positive cycle at the search cap therefore excludes
all smaller candidate periods. This strengthening uses the existing
constraint graph and introduces no additional enumeration dimension.

\subsection{Completed finite classification}
The original, rank-pruned and chain-separated searches all complete the
cap $168$, returning exactly the word $w_*$ of Appendix~\ref{app:witness}.
Their internal prefix counts are:

\toprule

\midrule

Each row completely searches every period not exceeding the cap.
Every run accepts the same single normalized $104$-letter word.
At cap $168$, chain separation reduces the rank-pruned prefix count by

The supplement retains all three enumerators as a fixed source and small
source-pinned overlays. Complete runs compare their accepted word lists.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
By Proposition~\ref{prop:upper}, every candidate has period at most $168$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:extreme} places it in the normalized event domain.
Proposition~\ref{prop:eventtest} and the complete enumeration cover every
candidate in that domain. The count, cycle, and chronology rejections above
cannot remove a realization. The complete output contains only $w_*$.
The independently checked finite seed realizes $w_*^\omega$ and identifies
it with a phase of the standard word. Thus existence and uniqueness both
hold. The two pruning refinements supply further completed traversals of the
same domain with the same unique output.
\end{proof}

\[
 \ind_B(q_i+kd)\ \mathbin{\mathrm{xor}}\
 \left(\#\{(j,s):q_j-q_i=sd,\ s>0\text{ or }(s=0,j<i),\
                    s\le k\}\bmod2\right).
\]
The seed term has finitely many hits, and the historical-write term changes

\section{Wake-dependent period bounds and inverse synthesis}\label{app:general}
The standard-wake classification is one instance of a general inverse
problem. Prescribe a non-axis drift $d$, an initial heading, and a finite
set $O\subset\Z^2/\Z d$ of odd classes. We seek a periodic word and finite
seed with exactly these data. The following bounds, developed in the first
version~\cite{previous}, determine a finite search range directly from the
input wake.

Lemma~\ref{lem:criterion} gives $\#\R-\#\Lturn=|O|=:g$.
Heading restoration implies $g\equiv0\pmod4$, $P$ even and $d_x+d_y$ even.
For $d=(m,-m)$, the complete class label is $(x+y,x\bmod m)$.
The checker-sector invariant $\sigma$ is determined by the supplied pose.

\subsection{An integer clock for diagonal budgets}
After rotating a diagonal drift parallel to $(1,-1)$, the transverse
coordinate $x+y$ and heading return after each period. A clock using these
quantities has a cancelling
head correction: its period gain comes from the wake. We recall the unit
increment identity of~\cite{clock} before computing that gain.

Fix an integer $c$, set $s=x+y-c$, and put
\[
 f(s)=3^{|s|}-1,\qquad
 \epsilon(h)=\begin{cases}+1,&h=N,E,\\-1,&h=S,W,\end{cases}\qquad
 G(s,e)=\begin{cases}\tfrac12 f(s),&es\ge0,\\-\tfrac32 f(s),&es<0.\end{cases}
\]
Use the integer clock of~\cite{clock} on the complete finite state
\begin{equation}\label{eq:integer}
 \Theta_c(X)=-\sigma(X)\sum_{q\in B}\chi(q)f(q_x+q_y-c)
             +G(p_x+p_y-c,\epsilon(h)).
\end{equation}
Since $f(s)$ is even, $\Theta_c$ is an exactly computable integer.

\begin{lemma}[Integer clock, Ke~\cite{clock}]\label{thm:clock}
For every finite-support state and integer centre $c$, with $e,e'$ the before
and after values of $\epsilon$ and $s=p_x+p_y-c$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gain}
 \Theta_c(FX)-\Theta_c(X)
   =1+4f(s)\ind_{\{e'\ne e,\ es<0\}}\ \ge1.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $\delta=+1$ on $\R$ and $-1$ on $\Lturn$. Directly checking the two colours
at four headings gives $\delta\sigma\chi(p)=ee'$, and the outgoing step gives
$s'=s+e'$. Thus the black-field increment is $-ee'f(s)$. Let $G_\pm(s)=G(s,\pm1)$.
The elementary recurrences are
\begin{align*}
 G_+(s+1)-G_+(s)&=f(s)+1,&G_-(s-1)-G_-(s)&=f(s)+1,\\
 G_-(s)-G_+(s)&=-2\sgn(s)f(s).
\end{align*}
If $e'=e$ the total gain is one. For $+1\to-1$ it is
$1+2f(s)+G_-(s)-G_+(s)$, equal to one for $s\ge0$ and to $1+4f(s)$ for $s<0$.
The other reversal gives the reflected cases. This includes $s=0$.
\end{proof}
\subsection{Diagonal wake budgets and crossing refinement}
Rotate a diagonal drift to $d=(m,-m)$, $m>0$, and use the transformed heading.
Let $t=x+y$, let $o_t$ count odd classes at level $t$, and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:A}
 A_t=-\sigma(-1)^t o_t,\qquad
 K_c(O)=\sum_t A_t(3^{|t-c|}-1).
\end{equation}
\begin{theorem}[Wake-determined diagonal period]\label{thm:diagonal}
Every realization of this wake obeys $P\le K_c(O)$ for every integer $c$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Over one period the head term of \eqref{eq:integer} cancels, since both $t$ and
the heading return to their initial values. The signed cell toggles give
$-\sigma\sum_i\chi(q_i)f(t_i-c)\delta_i$.
The weight is constant in each full $d$-class. Lemma~\ref{lem:criterion} makes
its net $\delta$ count one on odd classes and zero on even classes. The exact
period increment is therefore $K_c(O)$. Summing \eqref{eq:gain} proves the bound.
The unchanged part of the full field cancels algebraically; it is not deleted.
\end{proof}

Here is an independent geometric derivation, which also supplies a refinement.
The finite projected period is a closed walk with increments $\pm1$ in $t$.
Let $f_t$ count upward crossings of the cut $(t,t+1)$; it also counts downward
crossings. Let $m_t$ count visits to $t$, and $u_t$ count passages entering and
leaving in the increasing direction. Flow conservation equates this with the
number of decreasing-through passages. The fixed arrival axis and the two
turn choices give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:flow}
 m_t=f_{t-1}+f_t=A_t+4u_t,\qquad
 0\le u_t\le\min(f_{t-1},f_t).
\end{equation}
For example, at vertical-arrival levels, right turns are through passages and
left turns reverse the projection, giving $\#\R-\#\Lturn=4u_t-m_t$; horizontal
arrival reverses that sign. Substituting $u_t\le f_{t-1}$ into
$f_t=A_t+4u_t-f_{t-1}$ gives the first inequality below; $u_t\le f_t$
gives the second:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:recurrence}
 f_t\le3f_{t-1}+A_t,\qquad f_{t-1}\le3f_t+A_t.
\end{equation}
Let $\ell,h$ be the extreme wake levels. Outside them $A_t=0$; starting past the
finite support of the projected walk and applying \eqref{eq:recurrence} inward
forces $f_{\ell-1}=f_h=0$. Thus the periodic trace lies between the extreme wake levels. An empty wake forces every
$f_t=0$, impossible for a nonempty period.

Starting from these two zero boundary values, set
\[
 L_{\ell-1}=0,\quad L_t=3L_{t-1}+A_t,\qquad
 R_h=0,\quad R_t=3R_{t+1}+A_{t+1},\quad C_t=\min(L_t,R_t).
\]
Then $0\le f_t\le C_t$ for $\ell\le t<h$. If a $C_t$ is negative, the wake is
impossible; otherwise
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cuts}
 P=2\sum_t f_t\le2\sum_t C_t.
\end{equation}
Moreover $d_x=\sigma\sum_t(-1)^t f_t$: on a cut with $\sigma(-1)^t=1$, the
upward horizontal moves contribute $+f_t$ to $x$; on the other cuts the downward
horizontal moves contribute $-f_t$. If $C_+$ and $C_-$ sum the corresponding
positive and negative cut bounds, then also
\begin{equation}\label{eq:direction}
 P\le4C_+-2d_x,\qquad P\le4C_-+2d_x.
\end{equation}

\begin{corollary}[Width and growth bound]
Writing $W=h-\ell$, a diagonal realization has even $W$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coarse}
 P\le g(3^{W/2}-1).
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
At a transverse extreme every passage reverses the projection. The fixed arrival
axis makes all letters in an extremal class equal. Lemma~\ref{lem:criterion}
therefore forces exactly one $\R$ per such class. Both extremes have the same
arrival axis, so $W$ is even; this is the usual extremal rigidity argument.
Take $c=(\ell+h)/2$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:diagonal}, discard negative coefficients
and use $\sum_t\max(A_t,0)\le g$ to obtain \eqref{eq:coarse}.
\end{proof}

For the normalized standard wake, $\sigma=-1$ and the coefficients at
$t=-10,\ldots,0$ are
\[
 A=(2,-1,1,-1,1,-1,1,0,1,-1,2).
\]
The cut bounds are
$C=(2,5,16,47,142,145,48,16,5,2)$, with sum $428$.
Thus \eqref{eq:cuts} gives $P\le856$, as does \eqref{eq:A} with centre $c=-5$.
The chronological constraints in Section~\ref{sec:upper} strengthen this
budget to the cutoff used in the complete classification.

\subsection{General non-axis drift by reciprocal moments}
For a general drift, use a transverse linear coordinate constant along it.
An exponential weight and its reciprocal give complementary period budgets;
Cauchy--Schwarz then bounds the number of steps independently of their
individual weights.

Rotate the drift to $(a_0,-b_0)$, $a_0,b_0>0$. Put
$k=\gcd(a_0,b_0)$, $\eta=(b_0/k,a_0/k)$, and choose rational $r>1$ such that,
with $a=r^{\eta_x}$ and $b=r^{\eta_y}$,
\[
 \kappa=1-\frac2{a+1}-\frac2{b+1}>0.
\]
The choice $r=4$ always works. Define $w(q)=r^{\eta\cdot q}$, and
\[
 (\gamma_N,\gamma_E,\gamma_S,\gamma_W)
 =\left(\frac2{b+1},\frac2{a+1},-\frac{2b}{b+1},-\frac{2a}{a+1}\right).
\]
The one-sided potentials are
\[
 \Psi_+(X)=-\sigma\sum_{q\in B}\chi(q)w(q)+\gamma_h w(p),\qquad
 \Psi_-(X)=-\sigma\sum_{q\in B}\frac{\chi(q)}{w(q)}+\frac{\gamma_{h+2}}{w(p)}.
\]
Substitution of the current-cell flip gives $\Delta\Psi_+=\kappa w(p)$ in six
heading/colour cases and $(\kappa+4)w(p)$ in $S/\Lturn$ and $W/\R$.
The half-turn formula gives $\Delta\Psi_-\ge\kappa/w(p)$. These local identities
are the one-sided exponential clock construction, included to make the new
period argument self-contained.

Define the two finite wake moments, using any representatives of $O$,
\[
 A_+=-\sigma\sum_{q\in O}\chi(q)r^{\eta\cdot q},\qquad
 A_-=-\sigma\sum_{q\in O}\chi(q)r^{-\eta\cdot q}.
\]
They are independent of the representatives since $\eta\cdot d=0$ and
$d_x+d_y$ is even. The head terms cancel over a period, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:criterion} gives exact increments $A_+$ and $A_-$. Thus both are
positive and $\kappa\sum_i w(q_i)\le A_+$,
$\kappa\sum_i w(q_i)^{-1}\le A_-$. Cauchy--Schwarz yields:
\begin{theorem}[Reciprocal wake bound]\label{thm:moment}
Every realization with the specified non-axis drift and wake satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:moment}
 P^2\le \frac{A_+A_-}{\kappa^2}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
All quantities are rational. The integer square root of the floor of the right
side is a computable cutoff. Changing the transverse centre scales $A_+$ and
$A_-$ reciprocally, leaving the product unchanged. For axis drift the normal has
a zero coordinate, so this construction cannot give positive $\kappa$.

\subsection{All-period synthesis and detour obstruction}
\begin{theorem}[Period-unknown inverse synthesis]\label{thm:inverse}
Given a non-axis integer drift, an initial heading and a finite odd-class wake,
existence of a finite-support periodic realization is decidable. A positive
answer can produce its word and an explicit finite seed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
First reject odd $d_x+d_y$, growth $|O|$ not divisible by four, or a
nonpositive moment $A_+$ or $A_-$. Otherwise compute
Theorem~\ref{thm:moment}'s cutoff, additionally using
Theorem~\ref{thm:diagonal} and \eqref{eq:cuts}--\eqref{eq:direction} in the
diagonal case. A negative crossing bound also rejects the input.
Enumerate every nonempty word up to the least derived bound.
Check its heading and drift, apply Lemma~\ref{lem:criterion}, and require the
exact odd-class set. A passing word constructs a finite seed; complete finite
exhaustion gives a negative answer. Completeness follows from the period bound.
\end{proof}
The all-future verifier of Section~\ref{sec:verification} independently
checks the constructed finite seed. For cutoff $B$, enumeration visits at
most $2^{B+1}-2$ nonempty binary words; the period bound guarantees termination.

\begin{corollary}[No wake-preserving closed-pose insertion]
Suppose realizable words $u,v$ have the same non-axis drift, and $v$ is obtained
by inserting nonempty detours into $u$, each returning to the same position and
heading while leaving the original steps unchanged. Then
\[
 A_+(O_v)>A_+(O_u),\qquad A_-(O_v)>A_-(O_u).
\]
Thus their signed transverse wake cannot be identical. For diagonal drift,
$K_c(O_v)-K_c(O_u)\ge |v|-|u|>0$ for every integer $c$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Every retained original step has the same position, heading and letter; hence
it has the same local clock increment. Every inserted step has strictly positive
increment in both one-sided clocks, and at least one in the integer clock.
Sum over the words and use the exact wake-increment equalities.
\end{proof}
Thus any such realizable detour requires an additional wake budget.

\section{Certificate reconstruction specification}\label{app:certificate}
To make the finite upper lemma reproducible independently of a numerical
optimizer, the supplement specifies the following deterministic procedure.
Use bits $0$ and $1$ for right and left. Branch on the first read of a new
cell, and otherwise follow the colour left by its previous read. Explore
first returns to the starting cell of length at most $20$, pruning only
when the remaining unit moves cannot reach the start.
Append the original starting bit at the return, include complements, and
sort and deduplicate the resulting factors. Every proposed factor is then
rechecked for an equal-bit consecutive reread.

Insert all factor prefixes into a trie, remove forbidden contexts, and
use longest-suffix transitions. Place each retained context geometrically
relative to its endpoint for each heading. Intersect these placements with
the derived transverse interval and checker-sector constraint.
This produces the full graph of Section~\ref{sec:upper}.
Perform the degree-peeling reduction, regenerate the capped integer
potential, and rescan every retained edge.

For the exact stage, regenerate the opposite-direction minimum-cost tables
from \eqref{eq:counts}. Enumerate all arrival strings compatible with the row margins, perform the
fixed stable edge match, and apply the exact rejection rules in
Section~\ref{sec:search}. At the bottom, add the two singleton events before
testing for one final cycle; a legitimate final cycle must not be confused
with a prematurely closed component.
