\section{A sharp width-ten wake moment}\label{sec:moment}
Normalize the lowest transverse level to zero, and write $o_j$ for the number
of odd full-drift classes at level $j$, $0\le j\le10$. This is a translation of
the earlier top-zero convention; longitudinal lanes remain distinct.

\begin{theorem}[Sharp width-ten moment]\label{moment:thm:main}
Every complete finite-support periodic realization with full drift $(2,-2)$
and transverse width $W=10$ satisfies
\[
 \mathcal K=2\sum_{j=0}^{10}(-1)^j(j-5)^2o_j\ge168.
\]
Equality holds exactly for the standard period-104 turn word, up to cyclic
phase and the declared lattice normalization.
\end{theorem}
The theorem allows every growth, interior multiplicity and finite initial
field compatible with these geometric hypotheses. The proof derives its own
period bound separately for each wake. The standard profile attains 168.

\subsection{Finite-support colour chronology}
A phase $i$ in the drift class of $q_i$ can be written $q_i=b+a_i d$.
At the physical cell $b+nd$, for sufficiently large $n$, all phases of that
class are present. Their time order is by
\begin{equation}\label{moment:eq:stable}
\tau_i=i-Pa_i.
\end{equation}
Because the input black support is finite, such a far-ahead cell starts
white. Consecutive visits flip it. Hence the letters in every drift class,
ordered by \eqref{moment:eq:stable}, must alternate $R,L,R,L,\ldots$ starting $R$.
Conversely, the boundary colours at the finitely many truncated classes can
be chosen to realize any geometric periodic word obeying these alternations;
this exact criterion is due to Jillhewar~\cite{Jillhewar2026} and is also derived
in Lemma~\ref{lem:criterion}.

In particular, the signed excess $R-L$ in one drift class is $0$ or $1$;
it equals the indicator of an odd class. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{moment:eq:growth}
 g=\#R-\#L=\sum_j o_j\equiv0\pmod4.
\end{equation}
The end rails of a translating trace are geometric turning points. There
all visits to a given class must have the same required turn; alternation
forces exactly one white-first right turn. Consequently the extremal occupied
levels are also extremal odd-class levels and the incoming axis is horizontal.
Translation and the top-class phase fix a normalization with levels $0$ and
$10$, and two longitudinal lanes $r=0,1$.

Let $c_r$ count odd classes on even levels of lane $r$, and $q_r$ those on odd
levels. The signed mod-four residue restrictions and the two-lane mixed-wake
interleaving identity of Appendix~\ref{app:mixed} give
\begin{align}
 c_0-q_0&\equiv c_1-q_1\equiv c_0-q_1\equiv c_1-q_0\equiv2\pmod4,\label{moment:eq:mod4}\\
 V&=q_0+q_1\equiv0\pmod2,\qquad J+V/2\equiv1\pmod2,\label{moment:eq:mixed}
\end{align}
where $J$ counts ordered pairs of an odd class on an odd level and an odd
class on a higher even level. These are necessary tests, never a sufficient
realizability test. Previously established finite-support restrictions proved in the attributed residue analysis of Appendix~\ref{app:mixed}
ensure $g\in\{8,12,16\}$ at width ten; these restrictions reduce the possible growth without prescribing it as input.
For completeness, positive growth is divisible by four and there are at most
22 full classes. Growth four is excluded by the finite-support restrictions
summarized in Appendix~\ref{app:unit}. At growth twenty, the residue
conditions force six occupied even levels and four occupied odd levels in each
lane: all even levels then have both classes. Each odd class has an even number
of even classes above it, so $J$ is even; also $V=8$. This contradicts
\eqref{moment:eq:mixed}, leaving exactly the three stated growth values.

\subsection{An all-period bound from the wake}
The residue-independent forbidden-factor graph for this
width contains $54,718$ vertices and $90,158$ directed edges. Its integer
certificate consists of vertex potentials $y_v$ with
\begin{equation}\label{moment:eq:edge}
 y_v-y_{v'}+\alpha_j\epsilon\le-1,
 \qquad \alpha_j=-2(-1)^j(j-5)^2,
\end{equation}
for each edge $v\to v'$ from transverse level $j$, where $\epsilon=+1$ on
an $R$ and $-1$ on an $L$ turn. Every physically realizable periodic word of
width ten lifts to a closed walk in this graph. Importantly, the argument
covers walks with repeated vertices; it is not a simple-cycle-only bound.
Section~\ref{sec:upper} constructs this graph, proves coverage and gives
the integer inequalities. Those arguments do not assume a particular odd wake.

Summing \eqref{moment:eq:edge} over a true period eliminates the $y$ terms.
The stable-class alternation makes the total signed turn contribution at level
$j$ equal to $o_j$. Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{moment:eq:bound}
 P\le-\sum_{j=0}^{10}\alpha_jo_j=\mathcal K.
\end{equation}
This is an effective, wake-specific cutoff \emph{derived from}, rather than
added to, the hypotheses. If $\mathcal K<4$, no nonempty heading-restoring
candidate exists. To prove Theorem~\ref{moment:thm:main} it remains to exhaust all
permitted wakes with $4\le\mathcal K\le168$.

\subsection{Complete finite reduction}
\subsubsection{Wake masks and exact crossing-count relaxation}
Encode the odd set of each lane by an $11$-bit mask $a,b$.
There are exactly $496$ signed-residue-feasible masks for one lane.
Applying both extreme-level conditions, \eqref{moment:eq:mod4}, \eqref{moment:eq:mixed}
and \eqref{moment:eq:growth} yields $13,216$ normalized pairs. We do not bound a
black source's coordinates, nor choose a maximum length of a periodic word.

At one full class of transverse parity $j\bmod2$, let $I,J$ count its
incoming and outgoing ports on the already processed side. If $A$ of the
$I$ arrivals occupy even stable visit ranks and the class has odd indicator
$o\in\{0,1\}$, its total visit count is exactly
\begin{align}
 m&=2(I+J-2A)+o&&\text{on even }j,\label{moment:eq:evencount}\\
 m&=2(J-I+2A)-o&&\text{on odd }j,\label{moment:eq:oddcount}
\end{align}
with the necessary bounds
\begin{equation}\label{moment:eq:bounds}
 0\le A\le\lceil m/2\rceil,\quad
 0\le I-A\le\lfloor m/2\rfloor,\quad m\ge0.
\end{equation}
Matching port counts across adjacent layers supplies a four-integer boundary.
The dynamic program retains the minimum cumulative event cost at each complete
boundary, growth and horizontal displacement. Because future count constraints
use only these data, keeping the cheapest partial count history is exact for
this \emph{necessary relaxation}; no chronological realizability is inferred
from a count survivor.

All $13,216$ necessary masks are processed. Exactly $2,790$ pass the complete
integer port relaxation. When both top classes are present, choosing the other
top visit as origin exchanges longitudinal residues, yielding exactly $876$
paired duplicate masks. Thus $1,914$ canonical representatives remain.
Of those, $1,616$ have $\mathcal K\le168$ (Table~\ref{moment:tab:counts}).

\subsubsection{Physical chronology and completeness}
The count relaxation is not enough. The second stage attaches all actual
visit events in each full class in stable-rank order. Each $k$th ranked visit
has turn $R$ if $k$ is even, $L$ otherwise; its entering direction determines
its leaving direction. Directed-edge occurrences are matched first-to-first
in the stable order, as their lifted times differ by a fixed
$1-P\cdot\text{voltage}$ along a directed quotient edge. Each partial
object is therefore a union of directed chains. A premature closed component
is rejected, since it cannot later join the unique complete periodic cycle.

Let $z_A$ be a chain-root time, $p_i$ the position of event $i$ in chain
$A$ and $v_i$ its accumulated displacement voltage. Its stabilized time is
$z_A+p_i-Pv_i$. For consecutive stable ranks $i$ and $j$ on different
chains $A,B$, physical non-overlap requires
\begin{equation}\label{moment:eq:separation}
 z_B-z_A\ge \max\{4+p_i-p_j,\ell_A\}+(v_j-v_i)P,
\end{equation}
where $\ell_A$ is the length of chain $A$. For ranks in the same chain one
checks the direct gap four, not the cross-chain length. Each cross-chain
edge in \eqref{moment:eq:separation} has an integer constant term at least
$\ell_A>0$. Along a cycle of difference constraints the total bound has
form $C+KP$ with $C>0$. When $K\ge0$ positivity precludes every positive
period; when $K<0$ positivity at an upper cutoff $L$ implies positivity for
all $P\le L$. Thus testing a positive constraint cycle at the proved cap
$L=\mathcal K$ cannot wrongly reject a smaller real period. The within-chain
case follows by its direct monotonicity in $P$.

An incrementally maintained difference system applies this pruning after
each port addition and supports exact rollback. A separately constructed
root-variable constraint oracle rebuilds the entire partial problem and
checks the new incremental algorithm on selected streams. This is a scoped
implementation audit, not a replacement for the mathematical coverage
argument or for the complete enumeration.

At a fully matched object, acceptance requires \emph{one} directed phase
cycle, full voltage $+1$, horizontal drift $+2$, strictly increasing stable
ranks within every class, and exact heading restoration. Following the cycle
recovers a geometric turn word. Conversely, every realizable periodic trace
with the supplied odd mask creates exactly such a ranked event object; all
enumeration and pruning rules above preserve it. The entire future of an
accepted word is checked separately using complete finite-support parity
rather than inferred from a repeated finite prefix.

\subsubsection{Finite certificate and equality}
The machine-readable certificate lists the mask, growth, individual cap,
number of explored prefixes, and number of accepted words for \emph{every}
$\mathcal K\le168$ canonical representative. All $1,616$ searches terminated
with status \texttt{COMPLETE}. The accepted list has exactly one period-$104$ word, with the
standard $12$ odd classes. The aggregate explored-prefix count is
$767,594,212$; it is a code-coverage count, not an ant update count.

An independent ray-parity checker constructs a complete nineteen-black-cell
boundary source for that word and verifies its first required colour at each
phase. It tests the finitely many old-black-hit epochs, all self-write parity
thresholds and the final unbounded epoch interval. All $194$ required
phase/epoch checks pass. This establishes the positive realization and the
value $\mathcal K=168$.

Every candidate with $\mathcal K<168$ has no accepted word. At equality the
only accepted word is the standard phase. The cover and the derived cutoff
exclude any other periodic realization in the declared class at or below this moment value.
This proves Theorem~\ref{moment:thm:main}.\qed
