02 / Clock and escape

A clock that always advances.

The ant can turn back, revisit cells and erase its own trail. A quantity attached to the complete state still increases at every update. Inside a finite box, that clock has only a finite range.

Take one step to inspect its algebraic branch.

\[\Delta\Theta=1+\begin{cases}4(3^{|s|}-1),&e'\ne e,\ es<0,\\0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}\]

Here \(s=x+y-(n-1)\), and \(e=+1\) for north/east, \(-1\) for south/west. The displayed gain is recomputed from the full field, then compared with this local expression.

The interface evaluates individual transitions. The heading/colour identities and the finite-range argument establish the theorem for every state.

The proof in three moves

From one update to inevitable escape

1. Keep a parity invariant.

Let \(\chi(x,y)=(-1)^{x+y}\). The sign \(\sigma=\chi(p)\) for a vertical heading and \(-\chi(p)\) for a horizontal heading stays fixed: every update changes both position parity and heading axis.

2. Balance the field against the heading.
\[f(s)=3^{|s|}-1,\qquad J(s,e)=\begin{cases}f(s)/2,&es\ge0,\\-3f(s)/2,&es<0,\end{cases}\]\[\Theta=-\sigma\sum_{q\in B}\chi(q)f(q_x+q_y-c)+J(p_x+p_y-c,e).\]

Only the current cell changes colour. Its field contribution and the new heading correction combine to give an integer gain of at least one. Choose \(c=n-1\) for the square \(\{0,\ldots,n-1\}^2\).

3. Spend a finite clock range.

Restrict the sum to the square. Until the first exit, outside cells stay fixed and the same increment identity applies. The range is at most \(R_n=E(n)-1\). If the first exit is at update \(T\), the first \(T-1\) updates stay inside, so

\[T-1\le\Theta(X_{T-1})-\Theta(X_0)\le R_n,\qquad T\le E(n)=\frac{13\cdot3^{n-1}-2n^2-4n-5}{2}.\]

Long, thin rectangles behave differently

For fixed height \(H\), a separate rational clock gives a polynomial escape bound in the long side \(N\), of degree \(\lfloor H/2\rfloor+1\). The square clock above is useful for its exact universal increment; the rectangle proof chooses different weights.

What is established here?

The square and rectangle bounds are analytic theorems with exact algebraic checks. The repository also contains Lean proofs for the earlier supercritical local identities; the improved integer-clock square bound has its own analytic proof and exact checks.
