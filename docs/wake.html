Moving the period boundary changes the local view. The full displacement and number of classes visited an odd number of times remain fixed.

Why count odd visits?

Every visit flips a cell. An even number of visits cancels; an odd number changes its final color. The exact ordered reads within each translation class must alternate white and black. The exported seed satisfies the all-epoch color criterion, including the infinite tail.

The uniqueness theorem

    Why does a screen survivor remain a question?

    Parity and signed residue counts are necessary. They do not construct a consistent read ordering or prove that a proposed wake is realizable. The growth-eight survivor illustrates this distinction. The thin-wake classification requires the additional growth, width, and thinness assumptions shown above, with full drift fixed throughout.

    3. Follow the finite proof of an all-period statement

    Geometric hypotheses → finite count cases

    Full drift (2, −2), growth 12 and transverse width 10, with at most one odd class at each strict interior layer, constrain the layer occupancies. Integral crossing-flow equations reduce the possible counts to 76 cases. The standard profile and its residues emerge from classification.

    Integer clock → a certified period cap

    A finite window graph and 90,158 checked integer edge inequalities bound the period separately for each surviving count case. The bounds are proved before enumeration; the conclusion does not assume a period limit.

    Ordered reads → 83 exhaustive jobs

    The search enforces physical read alternation and exact full-drift classes. All 83 jobs are exhausted, visiting prefixes. Only equivalent standard highway representations remain. This page displays the resulting certificate data; it does not rerun that search in your browser.

    Surviving word → an infinite highway

    An independent all-epoch color verifier checks the survivor and synthesizes a complete finite seed. The period explorer above replays this exact seed. Together the bound, exhaustive exclusions and infinite-tail check establish the uniqueness theorem.

    Proof and reproducibility

    The general inverse-synthesis theorem also covers non-axis drift: a clock bounds candidate periods, ordered class constraints produce finite candidates, and the infinite-tail criterion accepts complete seeds.
