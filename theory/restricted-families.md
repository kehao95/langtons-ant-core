# Restricted-family program

Restricted theorems are useful only when their hypotheses make the boundary
between theorem and experiment unmistakable. Each family gets its own file,
finite data when needed, and verifier; no family is elevated by sharing a name
with a broader conjecture.

## First target: one black square

The smallest nonblank initial family consists of one black square, an ant at a
specified lattice point, and a specified heading. Symmetry can reduce the
number of presentations, but the reduction itself must state which rotations,
translations, or reflections preserve the update rule and how headings map.

A reconstructed one-black result must provide all of the following:

1. the initial state in the convention of `theory/problem.md`;
2. a finite sequence or independently checkable transition certificate;
3. the terminal predicate it reaches; and
4. an argument that that predicate implies indefinite highway evolution.

No such certificate is present yet. This section names a target and a required
proof shape; it does not assert the target has been met.

## Family expansion

Later work may organize finite initial black sets by a transparent measure,
such as support size, bounding-box geometry, or a history grammar. The measure
is an indexing device, not an induction principle, until a separate step
explains why all required predecessor states are covered.
